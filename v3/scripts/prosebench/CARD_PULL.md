# Card evaluation criteria — QUALITY · READABILITY · PULL (2026-08-24, from designer read of q9)

The card judged: `saves/web.json` q9 "Left Washing at Peatmoss" (real GUI session, gpt-5-mini,
shipped prompt). Verdict ~3.5/10. Three SEPARATE axes failed; keep them separate when scoring.

> At dusk the fort noticed washing left out for days at the loggers' winter huts in Peatmoss. The
> huts' foreman asks you to learn what kept the washer away and to bring proof or the washer's
> testimony. A small carved lizard that belonged to the washer is suspected to lie in a peat hollow
> near the huts. The huts' foreman at Peatmoss will pay coin. The coin is yours to keep.

---

## Axis 1 — QUALITY (is the writing alive?)
1. **The opener must be possible.** "the fort noticed washing [at a remote logging camp]" — the fort
   cannot see it. And "At dusk" (a moment) contradicts "for days" (a duration). The opener's job is
   *how this reached us*; a time-stamp standing in that slot is a defect.
2. **Nobody is a person.** Every human is a compound noun — *the huts' foreman*, *the washer*, *the
   loggers*. Nobody speaks, moves, or wants. Anonymity-by-trade was meant to withhold NAMES, not
   personhood.
3. **Dead verbs.** noticed / asks / is suspected / will pay / is — perception and state only. Nothing
   is picturable, and unpicturable text is slow regardless of word length.
4. **The best image is in the worst grammar, and it leaks the answer.** "A small carved lizard … is
   suspected to lie in a peat hollow" — passive, hedged, suspected by nobody; and it half-solves the
   mystery (keepsake in the bog ⇒ washer in the bog) while the job line never lets the player act on it.
5. **Pay sentences carrying zero information.** "will pay coin. The coin is yours to keep." Every job
   pays coin — that is a category, not a fact. "yours to keep" is a terms-of-service echo of the
   rewardEnvelope instruction, not a man promising something.
6. **job = the situation retyped.** "learn what kept the washer away … bring proof or testimony" →
   "Find what kept the washer from the huts and return proof or testimony." The field is wasted.
7. **Tense wobble**: noticed → asks → will pay, in five sentences, unmotivated.

## Axis 2 — READABILITY (cohesion, not sentence length)
The card scores WELL on every readability proxy (short sentences, plain words, one clause) and is
still effortful. The style rules are optimizing the proxies and destroying the real drivers.
1. **No pronouns for people, anywhere.** *the washer* ×3, *the huts' foreman* ×2, zero he/she/they.
   English signals "same person, keep moving" with pronouns; without them every mention re-identifies.
2. **Prepositional stacking.** "washing left out for days **at** the loggers' winter huts **in**
   Peatmoss"; "The huts' foreman **at** Peatmoss". This is where the load actually is.
3. **Cold definites.** "the washer" arrives already-definite though only *washing* was introduced —
   the reader back-infers the person from the laundry.
4. **ONE FACT PER SENTENCE obeyed literally ⇒ no connectives.** No so/but/because, no
   given-before-new hand-off. Five index cards, no thread; the reader does the assembly.
5. **Delayed subject-verb**, twice — "At dusk the fort noticed…"; an 8-word subject with a relative
   clause before "is suspected".
6. **Flat rhythm** — five identical NP+verb+complement declaratives, so nothing is emphasized and
   the eye has nowhere to land. The line the player needs does not stand out from the one they don't.
7. **Both high-attention positions wasted** — opener on an impossible perception, closer on
   "The coin is yours to keep."

## Axis 3 — PULL (designer ruling 2026-08-24)
**INTERESTING ≠ MOTIVATING.** Interesting = novelty/drama: expensive, cannot be on every card, and
demanding it from a cheap model yields purple mood-sentences (what "GAME WRITING, not literature"
exists to stop). Motivating = *I want to take this job*: cheap, structural, and REQUIRED on every
card **including the dullest routine contract**. A routine job may be uninteresting; it may never be
unmotivating.

Four sources — the card had none:
1. **A person who wants something, and what it costs them that it isn't done.** The foreman wants
   nothing and loses nothing. Give him a dozen loggers who won't work a camp with a vanished woman
   in it and the same job has a hand pushing it.
2. **A question the card REFUSES to answer.** Cheapest pull there is, and this card SPENDS it — the
   lizard tells me she's in the bog before I've hired anyone. Held open ("her lizard came up out of a
   peat hollow, and he won't say who found it") it becomes the reason to go.
3. **Something at stake for the COMPANY beyond coin** — the relationship, not the amount: a first job
   for a new client, a region that has never hired outsiders, a favor that will be owed.
4. **A reason to go NOW.** Routine jobs are infinitely skippable; one line of decay (the season ends
   at the thaw, the bog freezes) converts "sometime" into "this cycle".
5. **(designer's own) The card must make the player LOOK AT THEIR ROSTER.** If the prose names what
   the work will take in human terms — *someone who can get loggers to talk who don't like questions*
   — the player goes hunting for the right soldier and the choosing becomes part of the pull. Today
   the ask lives only in the dice fields and the prose invites nothing.

**The class-level requirement (checkable, purple-proof):** a card must show WHO WANTS IT AND WHAT IT
COSTS THEM, must leave EXACTLY ONE THING UNEXPLAINED, and must NOT hand the player the answer it is
selling. All three are FACTS, so they survive "every sentence gives the player something to use" —
the model is currently reading *human want* as mood and cutting it.

## Target (hand-written, same facts, same register, routine)
> The washing is still on the line at the winter huts in Peatmoss, four days grey with weather. She
> hung it out and never came back for it. The foreman has a dozen loggers who won't work a camp with
> a missing woman in it, and the season ends at the thaw — he wants an answer he can give them, and
> he'll pay for it. He'll say only that her carved lizard came up out of a peat hollow below the
> huts. He won't say who found it.

Nothing dramatic added, no adjectives, no mood: want, pressure and an open question are INFORMATION.
