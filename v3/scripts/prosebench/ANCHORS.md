# Prose bench — anchor texts (FROZEN 2026-07-18, before any baseline was scored)

Rule: anchors are never edited to move a baseline. Each anchor teaches the scale, not a diction
to imitate. A1/A3 are report-register (resolution prose); A2/B1 are card-register (briefings).
The matched anchor A1 runs ~1.5× its original's length — judges: length is NOT quality (RUBRIC).

---

## A1 — score 10 · MATCHED CONTENT (same game-generated facts as a real v3 artifact, rewritten by hand)

*The facts are identical to a real v3 resolution (Auline, the ford at Highford, the client's loaf,
a band squatting the feeder hollow, bluff + one show of steel, the night watch, the salted
pebbles and bare footprints toward Rimebarrow). Only the craft differs.*

> Auline came up on Highford at dusk, Sibbe's loaf still warm against her back. The carts told
> her the trouble before any man did: they stood axle to axle on the near bank, and no driver
> would be first past the fire in the feeder hollow below.
>
> She went down alone. A lean man rose and put his hand out for a toll; she looked at the hand
> until he put it away. "The ford works tonight," she said. "So do you, or you walk." She showed
> them steel once, and that was the whole of the fight. By dark their fire was ash and the first
> cart was creaking over the water.
>
> She kept the crossing till morning. At first light she found what the camp had been sitting
> on: a ring of pebbles, each one licked white with salt, and a line of footprints — bare feet,
> no boots — climbing toward Rimebarrow. Auline pocketed the pebbles and came home.

*Why 10: rhythm varies (a long establishing sentence, then a five-word turn); the confrontation
is a spoken line, not a summary ("spoke low and firm to the camp"); the details are SEEN
("licked white with salt", "bare feet, no boots"); every event is caused by the last; ends on a
person doing a small concrete thing. Zero purple words; still a session-log entry.*

## A2 — score 10 · PUBLISHED (Sultan's Game, verbatim card texts; designer's 10/10 reference)

> Junah is beautiful, but lacks a certain poise. This means she cannot charge as much, which,
> for many patrons, works just fine. The other prostitutes think she is wasting her potential,
> but they do not know what she wants.

> Card of the [tier], its texture [quality]. The image faintly depicts a scene of virtuous
> self-sacrifice, but alas, the woman's face has been erased.

*Why 10: three plain sentences, then a final clause that names a thing WITHHELD ("they do not
know what she wants"; "the woman's face has been erased"). Curiosity by omission. No adjectives
doing fake work; the last sentence is a hook aimed at the reader.*

## A3 — score 10 · PUBLISHED (Njáls saga, the bowstring; Dasent translation, lightly modernized)

> Then Gunnar said to Hallgerd: "Give me two locks of your hair, and you and my mother twist
> them into a bowstring for me."
> "Does anything hang on it?" she said.
> "My life hangs on it," he said. "They will never take me while I can keep them off with my bow."
> "Then I will remind you," she said, "of the slap you gave me. I do not care whether you hold
> out a long while or a short."
> "Everyone has some way of being remembered," said Gunnar, "and I will not ask you again."

*Why 10: dialogue IS the combat; the entire history of a marriage in five lines; total economy —
the register this game's world claims (laconic, dark, consequence-bearing).*

## B1 — score 8 · PUBLISHED (Fort of Chains, verbatim excerpts; designer's 8/10 reference)

*Setup-closer lines (each setup's LAST sentence aims at the player's decision):*
> A former hunter could be just the pet that you have always wanted.
> You could ignore it, of course, but this is practically money for nothing.
> Still, they were a former comrade, and would you leave them be just like that?

*A client's posting, in the client's own voice:*
> There are strange noises in my basement and I don't know what's going on down there. Reward
> offered to any brave souls willing to venture down, deal with the issue and restore my peace
> of mind.

*A failure outcome, deadpan:*
> "Call an exterminator. Maybe a professional rat catcher if there's one who specialises in
> giant rats." When asked the scale: "Small pony."

*Why 8 (not 10): the sentence craft is plain and the skeletons are visibly templates — but every
setup ends on a vector (temptation / justification / question), clients have voices, failure is
played for dry comedy, and named roster members appear inside the formula. Enjoyable, never dead.*
