# Widening the reference (2026-08-24) — designer: "sultan's game quality is excellent, but maybe
# there's others similar? the ones you pointed out seem too narrow."

Fair challenge: the ten laws in `REFERENCE_SULTANS_RESULTS.md` came from 4 samples of ONE game.
Below are two independent references in the same format, and the places where they DISAGREE with
Sultan's matter more than the places they agree.

---

# 1. Failbetter Games — the official Fallen London Writer Guidelines
A professional studio's house rules for exactly our shape: a storylet root, branches the player
picks, a stat check, and success/failure results.
<https://www.failbettergames.com/news/fallen-london-writer-guidelines-part-iii> (I and II also exist)

## Their hard word limits — INDEPENDENT CORROBORATION of the Sultan measurements
| Failbetter rule | our equivalent | Sultan's measured |
|---|---|---|
| Root description **≤ 30 words** | the card situation | median 24 (Spanish ⇒ English shorter) |
| Branch description **≤ 20 words** | the `job` line / approach label | slot text median 5 |
| Result description **≤ 100 words** | the resolution | median 51, p75 106 |

Two unrelated studios land on the same bands. **Card ≈ 20–30 words. Result ≈ 50–100 words.**
Our P26 cards run 50–75 — roughly double the card band. Our resolution caps (common 35/75, rare
60/125) are already about right.

## Their prose rules (close paraphrase of the guidelines)
- **Show, don't tell**: "evocative images and incident" are fundamental.
- **Do not put words in the player's mouth, thoughts in their head, or feelings in their heart.**
- **Dialogue must pass "the say-this-shit test"** — if it would not work spoken aloud, cut it.
  Direct speech is preferred over reported speech.
- **Period flavour is seasoning, not an ingredient** — "a little goes a long way".
- **Passive voice** sparingly; it drains energy.
- **Single-sentence paragraphs** used only to control pacing "come across as precious".
- Named cliché bans: diaphanous gowns, brief lives burning brightly, black as pitch,
  "it was quiet. Too quiet." · **"Pop Culture References: No."**
- Don't assume the player's gender, clothing, or skin colour.

## ⚠️ WHERE FAILBETTER CONTRADICTS SULTAN'S — and who wins for us
Sultan's writes the player's feelings directly: *"The clicking of coins make you smile, but then you
start to worry…"*. Failbetter forbids exactly that. **For our game Failbetter wins on a technicality
that resolves the tension cleanly**: our boss never leaves the fort, so there is no "you" in the
field at all. The emotional turn — which is the best thing in the Sultan samples — belongs to the
**SENT SOLDIER**, whose interiority we are free to write. Keep the turn; move it off the reader.

# 2. King of Dragon Pass — the closest MECHANICAL analogue we have
You are told a situation, you choose an approach, you pick which of your people to send, dice
resolve, you read the outcome. That is our loop almost exactly. Text via the wiki API, verbatim.

**"Attempted Theft"** — note the engine template slots, exactly like our dealt fields:
> Carls catch a man trying to sneak into a stead at night. He wears the insignia of clan <X>. He
> identifies himself as <Y>. Housed in the stead were a number of valuable items, including
> <treasure>.

**"Bear Trouble"**:
> For a few weeks, people have suspected a bear has been prowling around the small huts where sheep
> herders stay, and some of the barns. Now it has broken in to one of the sheds used for Women's
> magic. It has eaten most of the sacred grain, and destroyed urns, masks, and various ritual
> accouterments.

**"Bandit Infestation"**:
> The roads that connect the clans have become infested with bandits. Men outlawed from various
> clans have banded together, and are ambushing traders. They've taken two shipments of goods in
> the last season. They're probably attacking other clans, too.

## What KoDP adds that Sultan's did not show
1. **The "NOW" hinge.** Bear Trouble runs background → *"Now it has broken in to…"* → the specific
   damage. A long-standing nuisance becomes a JOB at a named moment. This is a better opening shape
   for routine work than either "an object is out of place" (ours) or "a person came to your door"
   (Sultan's), and it is the one our engine can actually feed, since we deal duration.
2. **Engine slots sit openly in the prose** (`<X>`, `<Y>`, `<treasure>`) — the reference is
   template-driven too. Our anxiety about dealt data appearing on the card is not shared by the
   craft reference; it is HOW it appears that matters.
3. **Short, flat declaratives, four of them.** No subordination, no ornament. Closest of all three
   references to something a cheap model can hit reliably.
4. **The last sentence widens instead of closing**: *"They're probably attacking other clans, too."*
   A speculation that makes the world bigger — a third closer type, alongside Sultan's dare and
   promise.
5. **Your own people can be the subject**: "Carls catch a man…" For us that is the fort's own
   patrol, the roster, the gate watch.

## ⚠️ KoDP CONTRADICTS one of my Sultan-derived laws
I proposed dropping the cost sentence (goal G4) because 0/4 Sultan intros account for cost.
**KoDP includes it**: "They've taken two shipments of goods in the last season"; Bear Trouble's
whole third sentence is the damage inventory. So **cost is not banned by the craft — it is banned
from being ABSTRACT.** The reference states cost as a concrete loss already suffered (two shipments,
the sacred grain, the urns and masks), never as our formula "he cannot tend his hives while he
watches it". Revise G4: keep cost, make it a thing already taken.

# CONVERGENCE — what all three references agree on
1. **Short.** 20–30 words for the card; 50–100 for the result.
2. **Open on people or an event, not on scenery sitting in a place.**
3. **Concrete nouns and incidents**; no adjective doing the work of a fact.
4. **Direct speech beats reported speech**, and only when the line does work.
5. **The last sentence points outward** — a dare, a promise, a widening rumour — never a summary.
6. **Numbers and mechanics live outside the prose.**

# DIVERGENCE — where the designer must choose
| question | Sultan's | Fallen London | KoDP |
|---|---|---|---|
| feelings in the reader's head | yes, and it is the best line in the samples | explicitly forbidden | no |
| cost of the trouble stated | never | n/a | yes, as concrete losses already taken |
| fragment stacks | used ("A nod, a word shared, a handshake") | discouraged as "precious" | not used |
| player's own people as subject | no (you act) | no (you act) | yes ("Carls catch a man") |

**Still missing across every reference: a FAILURE result.** Three Sultan successes, no loss, and the
wikis carry outcomes only as mechanics. Failure prose is historically our weakest class.
