## ✅ CONFIRMED — C4 (open on an animate subject)
**105 of 125 blocks (84%) carry an animate subject within the first seven words.** The exceptions
are almost all still animate-adjacent ("The roads that connect the clans have become infested with
bandits", "Someone has taken <treasure>"). Genuine scenery openings are close to nonexistent.
My P26 opens on scenery 18/18. This is now confirmed across three independent games.

## ❌ FALSIFIED — C3 (long, subordinated sentences)
Sultan's averages 15–29.5 words per sentence; KoDP/Six Ages sits at a median of **15.0** with p75 at
18. **Long sentences are a Sultan's Game house style, not a property of the form.** Since the
designer's readability ruling outranks everything, adopt the KoDP band (12–18 w/sentence) and treat
Sultan's longer rhythm as permitted, not required. My P26 cap of "none past fifteen words" was not
wrong so much as too rigid — it should be a centre, not a ceiling.

## 🔧 REFINED — C1 (the desire frame is not the only working frame)
I wrote C1 from Sultan's alone: cards offer something you might WANT, never a misfortune with a cost
attached. KoDP is full of misfortunes — and they work, because of HOW they are framed. Verbatim:

> Wild dogs rove your pastures, killing <number> goats.                                      [8 w]

> <X clan> warriors kidnap <Y>, daughter of the chief, and take her back to their tula.      [16 w]

> Alarm sweeps your lands as many of your horses fall sick with <specific illness>.          [14 w]

> The roads that connect the clans have become infested with bandits. Men outlawed from various
> clans have banded together, and are ambushing traders. They've taken two shipments of goods in
> the last season. They're probably attacking other clans, too.

Every one is **an aggressor acting on YOU or YOURS, in the present, with the damage counted.**
Active verb, animate subject, your property, ongoing.
Compare mine: *"A barred door hangs on Thornhollow's outer hall and has stood so since dawn."*
An object, sitting, in the passive, belonging to a stranger, with a tradesman inconvenienced.

**So the guideline needs TWO frames, and my restitution frame is neither of them:**
- **FRAME A — DESIRE** (Sultan's): something you might get, priced by difficulty or doubt.
- **FRAME B — INCURSION** (KoDP): someone or something is taking from you, now, and here is the count.
Both put an animate subject in motion. Neither is "an object is out of place and a stranger cannot
work while it lasts."

## Note on outcome prose
Neither wiki records success/failure BRANCHES as prose — the agent checked every heading across all
1,400 pages and found zero. The 24 outcome texts that exist are later in-game "report" screens
transcribed by editors. **We still have no reference failure text from any source.**

## Outcome craft in this corpus (25 blocks, 18–110 words, median ~31)
A genuinely DIFFERENT register from Sultan's, and both are valid:

> Shamans from the <X clan> came to help us. They awakened the spirits, who said that they would
> teach us many forgotten secrets of crafting, which would increase our success in trade.

> Although <X> and the expedition never returned, the ancestors said that they had died fighting
> trolls, our ancient enemies. It must have been a mighty battle, because their sacrifice
> strengthened our magic.

> Remains of <Name>, the warrior we exiled for beating our elders, have been found not far from
> here. It is hard to say but he was probably killed by neighboring patrols.

> The <Clan A>s never launched that raid against the <Clan B>s. They found no one willing to help
> them by striking first. That leaves the <Clan B>s in possession of that annexed territory.

What it does differently from Sultan's:
1. **Collective first person — "we / us / our".** The outcome is the clan's own report of itself.
   Sultan's uses second person ("you smile"). For us the analogue is the COMPANY: the party came
   back and this is what the fort now knows. Worth a designer look — it may fit our fiction better
   than either, since the boss stayed home and hears a report.
2. **Deadpan, and willing to be UNCERTAIN**: "It is hard to say but he was probably killed by
   neighboring patrols." No drama, no emotional turn. This is the closest thing to a FAILURE
   register anyone has produced across all sources.
3. **A causal clause does the meaning-making**: "It must have been a mighty battle, **because**
   their sacrifice strengthened our magic." The outcome explains itself in one subordinate clause
   rather than ending on an image.
4. **Outcomes may report a NON-EVENT** and its consequence — the raid that never came, the relations
   not bettered. The world moved without you and you are told what that leaves standing.

**Two registers now on the table for results:** Sultan's sensory-and-feeling close, and KoDP's
plain collective report with a causal clause. The designer's samples are Sultan's, so Sultan's is
the target — but the KoDP register is the one that plausibly handles FAILURE without melodrama,
and failure is our weakest class.
