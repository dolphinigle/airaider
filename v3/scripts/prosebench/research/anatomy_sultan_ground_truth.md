## What this overturns in my current prompt (P26)
1. **SENTENCE COUNT.** Reference intros are **1–3 sentences** (1, 3, 2, 1). P26 mandates exactly
   FOUR. My four-sentence rule is the single biggest structural mismatch with the goal.
2. **SENTENCE LENGTH — my cap is backwards.** P26 says "none past about fifteen words". The
   reference AVERAGES 15–29.5 words per sentence in intros, with individual sentences at 22, 22 and
   37 words. The reference writes FEWER, LONGER, flowing sentences; I generate MORE, SHORTER, flat
   ones. That is very likely what "not great" means — my cards read as chopped declaratives.
3. **TOTAL LENGTH is bimodal, not a single band.** Intros are either ~15–22 words (one sentence,
   samples 1 and 4) or ~54–59 words (two or three sentences, samples 2 and 3). There is nothing in
   between. A single fixed word budget cannot produce this; the SIZE is a property of the quest.
4. **Success texts: 19–53 words, 1–5 sentences.** Consistent with Failbetter's ≤100 rule and with
   the measured Sultan settlement median of ~51 (Spanish).
5. **The pre-roll addition scales hardest** — 15, 121, 34 words. Sample 2's is longer than its own
   intro and success combined; that is where the scene actually lives.

## Cross-check against the two corroborating references
- Failbetter's published limits: root ≤30 w, branch ≤20 w, result ≤100 w. Sample 3's 59-word intro
  BREAKS their root rule — so the two references genuinely differ on card length, and the designer's
  samples are the tiebreak. Treat ~15–60 words as the card range, not ≤30.
- Sultan's full-corpus measurement (Spanish translation, 1485 intros): median 24, p75 39, p90 60 —
  which brackets all four designer samples (15, 22, 54, 59) and confirms the bimodality is real
  rather than an artefact of a four-sample view.
