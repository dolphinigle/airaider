# WHAT TRANSFERS FROM SULTAN'S GAME — AND WHAT DOES NOT
Written from a complete read of the corpus: 389 job-like cards (read by me), 530 non-job cards in the
lower half and 555 in the upper half (read in full by two agents). ~1,470 cards, ~42,000 words.
This is the actionable output of the research. Where a claim has a number, the number is from a count.

---
# THE RULE WITH THE HARDEST EVIDENCE: body = fiction, slots = arithmetic
- **3 of 555** upper-half card bodies contain a digit. **8** mention "gold coin".
- Meanwhile **109 of 555** carry a gold-coin cost **in their slot labels**, **83** carry a
  "must do this yourself" requirement in slots, **49** a "break a Card of tier X" requirement.
- Lower half agrees: only **18 of 429** bodies mention mechanics at all.
**The division is near-absolute. The body says who wants what and why; the slot line says what it
costs and who it takes.** Five independent readers/writers reached this conclusion separately.
We currently write only bodies, and push cost, count and requirement into invisible dice fields.

# THE SINGLE HIGHEST-VALUE TECHNIQUE, and it fits a mercenary game exactly
**Give the client their own self-serving justification, then undercut it in the last clause.**
> Client's reason: *"She's a nomad half-breed who doesn't believe in the True God, which is already
> affecting my business, and she refused to change her faith!"*
> Narrator's undercut: *"In truth, it was no secret that his fortune was amassed from selling
> nomadic specialties..."*
Every client lies about why they want the job done, and the card can say so in eight words. This
needs no continuity, no recurring cast, and no shared history — it works cold on a one-off card.

# THE FIX FOR OUR MONOTONY PROBLEM
**Individuate the job by the CLIENT'S MOTIVE, never by the craft.** Jenna's fourteen jewellery
commissions are all "make jewellery". What differs is why: a vain lord wants the stone to look as
large as possible · a woman about to give birth wants a blessing for the child · an old eunuch needs
slaves of the Sultan to wear something worthy of his house. Same task, fourteen distinct cards.
This maps directly onto our "escort a caravan" ×40 problem, which no amount of prose variation has
fixed.

# ALSO TRANSFERABLE (each observed across the whole corpus)
- **Open on an actor with a want, in ROLE not name** — *"A wealthy merchant finds you, asking…"*,
  *"A young lord sent Jenna an order…"* This is a job-board opening and it works with zero continuity.
- **End on an open hinge, never a summary** — a question handed back (50 endings) or a stop before
  the choice (61).
- **The 6-to-12-word card is a legitimate form**, not a failure: *"Rumor has it, Nawfal is missing."*
  *"They starve."* *"This will not be easy."* Length is BIMODAL — a short spike and a long tail — not
  a house length. Use the short one as a punchline instrument.
- **Scare quotes as the euphemism tell** (37 cards): *"You could "lend" it…"*, *"a generous
  "tribute""*, *"this "sacred blessing" has fallen upon you"*. Signals a lie without editorialising.
- **One concrete physical detail instead of an adjective pile**: *"His throat moves as he swallows."*
  *"walks with a slight tilt to one side, as if he's spent years carrying a sword at his hip."*
- **Identical prose + a changing slot line as an escalation device** — seven "Ascension of Lamblet"
  cards are word-for-word identical and differ only in the slot ("You have 7 rounds left" → "This is
  your last chance."). Free tension from repetition; deliberate, not lazy.
- **Let the good option cost something**: *"Theoretically, the True Faith does charity, but clearly,
  it's not free."* (11w) — refuses a clean option in one clause rather than a paragraph of moralising.
- **Name a REASON, not just a task.** Bare-task cards were independently identified as "the dead
  ones" by both full-corpus readers.

---
# DO **NOT** IMPORT — fiction-bound, and three of these we were already copying
**1. Serialized-companion density.** 255 of 555 cards name a recurring NPC; Samir has 21 cards,
Nawfal 22. That is why *"Samir continues his search for the truth—one he has long refused to face."*
(13w) works: twenty other cards paid for it. **A jobs game with one-off clients cannot borrow this
sentence type — the same construction about a stranger is empty air.** This finally explains why our
cards feel thin next to theirs, and it is NOT fixable by prose.

**2. The "you must do this personally" grammar.** 83 cards carry a "yourself / in person" slot, and
the prose reinforces it. **The corpus's protagonist is a person who DOES things; a mercenary company
ASSIGNS people. The grammatical centre of gravity is different, and lifting this phrasing will make
our prose fight our loop.** ⚠️ We have been doing exactly this.

**3. Ellipsis-as-punctuation.** 171 of 555 cards (31%). **This is a Chinese-source translation
artefact (`……`), not English craft**, and at that density in native English it reads as evasive
mush. ⚠️ I had been treating it as a device. Take the underlying move — stop before the choice — at
perhaps a quarter of the frequency.

**4. The transgression subsidy.** Slavery, harems, cult sacrifice and licensed predation do enormous
work for free. *"just 5 Gold Coins, and this ghastly-looking Old Female Slave will be yours"* is
chilling because of the institution, not the sentence. Copying the shock register without the
institution reads as posturing.

**5. Title-case ruleset nouns in body prose** (Bloodshed Card, Conquest Card, Aether, Notoriety).
Looks like worldbuilding; is a glossary. Importing our own equivalents would hollow the writing out.

**6. The single fixed antagonist and doom clock.** 70 cards mention the Sultan, and much of the wry
voice is a joke about the countdown. We have no equivalent single pressure, so the humour will not
transfer with the phrasing.

**7. Register-locked-by-track.** Works there because the game has stable tracks (food / court / cult
/ charity), and the wry voice is QUARANTINED — it never appears on an atrocity card. For
heterogeneous one-off jobs this gives nothing unless we lock register per JOB TYPE, which is a
design decision, not a prose one.

**8. The corpus's surface errors.** "You can go youselft", "A Daughtet's Tale", "You must provide18
Gold Coins", double periods, two cards shipped in untranslated Chinese. The IDEAS are strong; the
SURFACE often is not. A model prompted on raw text will reproduce the typos as style.
