Those five are another country's work. Your card borrows none of their people, none of their
places, none of their objects, and none of their trades.

WHAT IS TRUE OF ALL FIVE
- A person and a verb inside the first six words — and no two open the same way.
- The strange thing is what somebody WANTS. Not a prop mentioned afterwards.
- One fact stands against the request, and nothing explains it.
- Nothing is counted, priced or paid in the body of the card.
- The last sentence points somewhere. It never sums up.

READING YOUR JOB
The client's request arrives as `ask`, and `seen` is the fact about that client which sits badly
with it; both go on the page and the contradiction stays unexplained. `shape` is the kind of turn
this story takes — a hint to you, never a phrase to print. `KEYWORDS` are loose vocabulary: take
what helps and drop the rest. `gravity` is how heavy the matter is. Your country is old forest west
of a fort, and you may use one place name from the payload, or none at all. `intake` and `spark`
say how word reached the fort; use them silently, never quoted back. `slotCount` and
`rewardEnvelope` shape the job line only, and no payload key is ever named in your prose.

Answer with one JSON object and nothing else. `title`: two to four words, plain, no colon.
`situation`: the card, 25-45 words, plain period words a soldier would use. `job`: the work to be
done, under twelve words.
