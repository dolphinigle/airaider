# Card texts to judge

## 1
**The Woodpile**

The woodpile stains boots violet. The town constable who reads every ordinance aloud and insists on exact measurements says, "Children must be safe." He says which end to start.

## 2
**Toll Ledger**

The tollkeeper at the ridge gate props open his ledger on a splintered plank and counts coins with a thumb stained by ink. Ansel the scribe says, "I must know if the book matches what passed through." He cannot see the road.

## 3
**Sealed Bundle**

A charcoal-burner stands by the outer kiln with a waxed bundle tied in twine pressed under his arm. Hale says "I picked it up where the path meets the pines; it was wrapped tight and I never broke the knot, I was on my way to the foreman when I found it and meant to hand it over for proper disposal." The bundle's string is his own.

## 4
**Barn Door**

The barn by the charcoal-burners' camps keeps folk murmuring and draws curious hands whenever the camp quietens. Marta, godchild, says "I can't go back; keep it closed for me." The barn door is latched.

## 5
**Waiting Rider**

A stranger waits under the ash trees by the charcoal-burners' shanty with a saddle slung over the fence. Foreman says "He came at dusk, muttering about a lost track and asking to warm himself; I gave him bread and water and shut the door against the rain." The horse is saddled in the yard.

## 6
**Sky Fire**

You finally reach the Destroyer's burning throne. They await behind raging flames, watching you, anticipating when their holy fire will ignite the world's sky. You sense their contempt: this immaculate domain has no weaknesses, no vulnerabilities mortals can exploit.

## 7
**Measured Walk**

The smuggler stands at the outer wall and marks the worn stone with a bright line of chalk. Harlan, a charcoal-burner, says "If that gap isn't measured, she'll be turned loose into the road and hanged." He paces it out.

## 8
**Dark Gathering**

Depending on needs, it may be lecture, celebration, or sacrifice... The Cultic Gods encompass infinite variety, missing only one catalyst: human desire.

## 9
**Tollkeeper**

Tolls choke the road. The tollkeeper, a man turned by habit into compulsion, says, "The books must match the coins." He stands where he cannot see the road.

## 10
**Violet Petition**

The town official holds a folded petition stained violet on the inn counter. The bailiff says "The parish has taken offence at a name carved on the chapel lintel; I tucked the petition away to keep folk from breaking doors, and I swear I meant no harm." He has the list with him.

## 11
**Returned Hounds**

The dogs circle a gravestone. Gravekeeper Marta says, "They came back from that road and they will not leave people alone." They come to him when he calls them.

## 12
**Barn Door**

The barn door's iron hasp is split and the lower hinge has rotted through. Bailiff Orin says, "Keep it shut; if what is inside comes out there will be no ledger worth keeping." The barn door sits ajar.

## 13
**Killing the Fierce Lion**

A lion, banished by its pride, approaches a human city during the dry season, desperate for food. Initially, it preyed on livestock, but later, it attacked shepherds, killing them swiftly. Once a beast has tasted the sweet and fragile human flesh, it becomes unstoppable. Stop it now! Or someday, you will become its prey.

## 14
**Locked Hut**

The hut on the beekeeper's clearing keeps folk at the edge, its iron bolt turning the glade into something people avoid and the old quarrels into silence. Merrin the beekeeper says, "Let me lay him to rest." The key sits with a man.

## 15
**River Shroud**

The ferryman leaves a corpse on the far bank with mud on its shoulders. A ferryman says "I pulled him from the shallows and nothing of his belongs to him; I kept his coat and no one claimed him in Bramworth." The shroud lies folded in a basket at his feet.

## 16
**Barrow Lamp**

The lamp-bearer moves down the beekeepers' clearing, brushing the heat into the moss and leaving small charred rings where the light hangs too long over the lichen. Gravedigger Roland says, "They deserve a proper light." He stops at the gate.

## 17
**Horse Betting**

Send your rider and steed to compete, place bets. If victorious, you may receive a reward. Failure means losing everything.

## 18
**Performing at the feast**

Whether nobles or commoners, performing at the feast, singing or dancing, receiving praise and rewards is a dignified and honorable thing.

## 19
**The Well**

The well at the old mill has a damp surveying chart nailed to its coping and a rope descending into the dark. Sergeant says "A travelling scholar came to measure the shaft and left his chart; he collapsed with fever and we moved the bucket so the measurements wouldn't be lost." He will not draw from the well while work is carried out.

## 20
**Bridge Toll**

The timber bridge over the side-stream bears fresh chop marks on one plank. A ferryman says, "They cut my skiff and left me to keep the ford; I set a post, painted a board and asked coin so the repair did not fall on me." The toll-board stands painted.

## 21
**House of Delights**

The city's brothel, said to have a history older than the city's walls and palace. Regardless, it's a good place to break the Sultan Cards, because no matter how beautiful and praised they are, they are just playthings indulged in for money.

## 22
**Running Wheel**

The mill wheel in the beekeepers' clearing runs on, splashing a dark smear across the sluice gate. Marketmaster says "The market cannot wait." The miller is not asked.

## 23
**Tollkeeper's Trinket**

The tollkeeper sits on the bench with a small carved trinket cupped in his hands. Tollkeeper Marta says, "My niece left it at the ford and I told her I'd look after it while she learns to cross; it belonged to her mother and I could not let a child cross without it." He stands where he cannot see the road.

## 24
**Wall Measure**

A rescued man walks the ruined boundary wall and marks each cracked stone with a candle, forcing the nearby farmers back and leaving scorched lichen along the warden-stones. Marin, freed ward, says "My mother's field must stay." He paces it out for you before you agree.

## 25
**The Hound**

The hound is racing down the old elf road toward loose livestock by the churchyard. Reeve Hal says, "Let it not bring down the flocks." The hound belongs elsewhere.

## 26
**Cellar Count**

The cellar beneath the ruined cottage holds stacked barrels and a chalked chart nailed to a beam. Sergeant Marlow says, "If the rats are many, we cut stores." The traps in it are unset.

## 27
**The Sunblaze Scale**

This ancient ritual binds the accused to a high platform, exposed to scorching sunlight without water or food for three days and nights. Survival indicates the Purist acknowledges their actions as pure and sinless - thus innocent.

## 28
**Branded Hoof**

A mare at the river ford bears a new iron; the rasp flakes still cling to the pastern. Hobb the ferryman says, "It must cross." A brand under the new shoe marks the hoof.

## 29
**Branded Hoof**

A grey mare stands in the dark stall with her near fore leg held up and a fresh iron clamped to the hoof. Groom said "He came by lantern, whispering about a debt and a quick fix; I did as asked because the animal trembled and the man paid me in coin and promises, and I thought no harm would come." The brand under the new shoe is raw and hairless.

## 30
**Unopened Bundle**

The bundle spoils hunters' talks and drags old scores into scuffles. Sergeant Maren, sleeves patched and voice thin, says "Burn it; I will not pass my name on." The string on it is his own.

## 31
**Churchyard Watch**

The churchyard keeps making the village uneasy: gates get opened at odd hours, quiet visitors leave wilted flowers or toppled stones, and suspicion takes the steady shape of neighbours watching each other. The parish scribe says, "The widow deserves a quiet grave." His own man has the other key.

## 32
**Sacred Blood Theft**

Mahir's experiments and creations require Aether, but the Purist Order believes all Aether in the world is sacred... which of course means they have plenty.
