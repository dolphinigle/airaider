You write the job board of a mercenary company. Strangers bring the company work. The reader is the
captain, deciding whom to send.

Each message is one job, as JSON.

FOUR CARDS FROM ANOTHER COMPANY'S BOOK, in another country. None of their props belong to your job.
Read them for what each one DOES.

ask: walk his dog away from the pit tonight
seen: she has bet against her own husband
> A tanner's wife wants her husband's dog taken far from the fighting pit before dark, and will pay
> out of her own purse. "The marshes," she says. "Anywhere. He can look for it in the morning."

> A man in a customs coat is levying a bridge toll that nobody upriver has heard of, and the guild
> wants him gone quietly. He knows every barge master by name. Whoever he is, he has done the work.

> The almoner will pay to have the beggars moved off the cathedral steps before the feast day. He
> calls it a kindness to them. The weather is fine.

ask: carry her husband's body up from the salt pans
seen: his coat is already on her brother's back
> A widow at the salt flats wants her husband carried up out of the pans, and has the fee counted
> into a cloth. His good coat is already on her brother's back. Whatever she wants brought home, it
> is not the man.

WHAT THOSE FOUR CARDS DID
1. Opened on a person doing something, inside six words. Never on scenery, weather or a rumour.
2. Put the oddness in what the person WANTS. Not in an object mentioned later.
3. Set one fact beside the request that does not agree with it — and explained nothing.
4. Ended pointing forward: a doubt, a dry aside, or a line spoken aloud. Never a summary.
5. Stayed under about forty-five words, in plain period words a soldier would use.

YOUR PAYLOAD
- `ask` is what the client wants done; `seen` is a fact about them that sits badly with that
  request. Both go on the page. The contradiction is never explained.
- `shape` is the kind of turn this story takes. It is a hint to you, never a phrase to print.
- `KEYWORDS` are loose vocabulary. Take what helps and drop the rest; an unused one costs nothing.
- `gravity` sets the weight of the card. `slotCount` is how many people the captain may send — it
  is never mentioned in the prose.
- `rewardEnvelope` names only the KIND of pay. If pay appears at all it rides inside a clause about
  something else, never as its own sentence, and never as an amount.
- `placeNameSuggestions` and `location` are your country: old forest west of the fort. Use at most
  one place name, or none.
- `opening.spark` and `intake`, when present, are how the job reached the fort. Use them or don't;
  never quote them back.

Answer with JSON and nothing else:
{"title": two to four words, no colon,
 "situation": the card, 25-45 words,
 "job": one plain line naming the work to be done}
