# R2 (probe4) against pre-R2 (probe3): the storyteller structure fixes, with Sonnet writing both arms, 2026-10-03

## 1. Verdict

R2 worked on the classes it targeted, but the structure is not right yet. Three of its own fixes went too far, and they cost followability on the middle jobs.

**What clearly improved (paired over 24 slots, Opus seat unless marked):**
- **Want-next (M3)** rose +0.53 (Opus) and +0.31 (Sonnet seat). Both are significant. M3 is now base +1.09, which is the G3 bar.
- **The answer is earned (S1).** J2 calls it tacked on in 6 of 24 sagas (9 with a looser reading), against 21 of 24 before.
- **The ending menu is gone (S3).** It appeared on 24 of 24 finale cards before and 0 of 24 now.
- **Part tags off-job (S4)** fell from 7 to 0.
- **Retries (S5)** are now marked as another try on 8 of 8 retry cards, and "new to you" is gone.
- **Spoilers (M12a)** fell from 13 to 5. Card-1 spoilers fell from 8 to 2 (significant). Spoilers coming from `why` fell from 11 to 0.
- **Finale cards** are followed on 24 of 24 (M1 finale 100%).
- **Caps held (M15)** rose +10.7pp (significant).

**What got worse:**
- **Rereads.** The Opus seat alone rose from 74.2% to 89.9% (+15.7pp, significant). On reports it rose from 61.8% to 83.1% (+21.3pp, significant). Both seats together (M5) rose from 59.0% to 69.7% (not significant).
- **Middle-card ease** on the Sonnet seat fell 0.76 (significant). M1-said on middle cards fell 24pp (significant).

**The causes are R2's own changes:**
1. **`why` now says too little.** It no longer says how the job serves the goal. The Opus reader wrote "unclear" on 23 of 41 middle cards (14 of 41 before); about 17 of those 23 are "how this job helps: unclear".
2. **Clue lines (`learn`) are cryptic fragments.** 18 of 51 middle-report rereads quote the clue.
3. **The prompt's glosses are printed as sentences.** Examples: "You hold X." / "X's fate is decided here." / "you have not met" / "You try this job again." Engine-speak quotes rose from 35 to 49.
4. **Held items are recited as a list on the finale card.** This happens on about 18 of 21 finale cards that hold 2 or more items. It replaced the ending menu.

**G1 on the R2 arm is still not met.** It now fails on paperwork (32.3%, unchanged), spoilers (5), the verifier (not re-run on the R2 prompts), caps (83%) and cost.

**What still fails, by class:**
- **Followability of the middle:** the `why` overcorrection (S6 / N-f) and cryptic clues (N3) drive the rereads and "unclear".
- **Machinery in prose:** gloss echo (N1) and held-item lists (N2). They replaced the menu and the part tags.

- **Not structure, still open:** paperwork 32%, caps 83%, verifier not run.

**Does the structure look right?** The spine is now right: one piece learned per win, gains held, and a finale that uses them and lands the answer. That is visible in M3 (+0.53*, base +1.09) and in J2 crediting a clue chain in 11 sagas. What is wrong is how three R2 fields are worded and rendered: `why`, `learn`, and `have`/`fate`/`retry`/`intro`.

**Candidate fixes, each a class (for Fable to rule; none applied):**
- **`why`:** the job's link to the want, meaning what the thing or person is and what having it lets the asker do. Never what the win uncovers.
- **`learn`:** a complete plain fact a stranger understands now, never a fragment. For a who-question, it narrows and never names.
- **Card glosses:** say what to cover, not a sentence to copy.
- **`helps`:** reaches the finale report (shown used), not the card.
- **Truth:** comes out inside `after` when it is found during the action.
- **The showdown and fates:** must still fit if the learned pieces turn the opponent into the wronged party.
