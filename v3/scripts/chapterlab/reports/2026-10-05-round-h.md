### Classes, with quotes

- **The readers credit the past as stakes in keep.** 42 of HP's 74 keep wins cite a death, a debt or a blame, against 18 of TC's 49:
  - *"A dead brother and starving widows give it weight."*
  - *"Selagus owes the beekeeper who hid him in her hives when raiders came."*
  - *"Pellecia's father died in the last burning and her stall is all she has left."*
- **S10 does not move.** HP's S10 losses are answers that do not add up, the same plan-level class every arm has:
  - *"The answer depends on a tinker we never meet."*
  - *"Rautio crouches beside the company at the hives, yet in the finale he turns out to be the raider."*
  - On F3_3 the burned-barn retry contradiction: *"The barn is 'lost to the flames' in report_1, yet card_2 asks you to hold it again."*
  - This is round T's TA/TB profile again: keep without a better story.
- **The past is the premise again, not a person's arc.** I read all 63 HP pasts:
  - **48** are the loss behind the want. *"Last year thieves tore open his hives at Fernholt and left the bees to die in the rain."*
  - **10** are a blame others put on them. *"…her people have blamed her ever since."*
  - **5** are a kindness they owe. *"When Selagus was a boy, a beekeeper hid him in her hives…"*
  - **None** is a wrong the asker did.
  - The `change` is the want achieved in almost every plan: *"Yayone has sold her flock at Falshaw market and keeps her farm."*
  - So card 1 says the want twice, and the finale's change is the ending we already had.
- **Card 1 loses its open question,** as PP and line did. TC states it on 56 of 63 first cards, HP on 47. The past takes its room (both cards average 85 words). The quoted voice line on card 1 goes from 63 of 63 to 7.

### Principle

PP worked because the soldier's past is **dealt**: their backstory and dossier hand the writer a wrong they did. A hired asker has nothing dealt, so when the plan is asked to invent a past, it writes the premise again. This is the dealt-mandate law: a person's own wrong must come in as an item (a seed, a dossier, a returning asker's memory), never as a request in the prompt.

## 3. RF: the retry reads the failure (HOLD part (a), drop part (b))

**What it does, as generated (after the verifier fixes):**
- **(a)** A failed job's report gets the engine fact `stands`: *"whoever and whatever the job names, and its place, for the company tries this job again"*. The gloss is "what still stands when it ends". The "tries again" clause is dropped when no retry follows.
- **(b)** The retry card gets only the failure and the job (no why, no trouble, a cap sized to the data). The retry report gets no hope.
- There are no "don't" rules.
- RF changes only the failure path. On draws 1–2 (no failures) it sends TC's exact prompts, so it is judged on the 8 draw-3 slots × 3 generations = 24 sagas.

### Retry contradictions

One blind counter per arm folder: a failure report establishes something (destroyed, taken, captured, lost), and the retry card or report then says otherwise. 27 failures per arm.

|---|---|---|---|
| TC | 6 | 4 of 24 | 4 |
| HP (same failure path as TC) | 4 | 3 of 24 | 3 |
| **RF** | **2** | 2 of 24 | **0** |

- **TC's contradictions:**
  - The hare is let loose, then sits in a basket on the next card (g1 F1_3).
  - The bargeman is hanged, then rescued from the noose (g1 F7_3).
  - The barn burns, then is whole, then *"Eraldil's warhorse stayed safe in her barn"* (g2 F3_3).
  - The finale stakes treat the burned barn as not yet lost (g3 F3_3).
- **HP's:** *"The barn was lost to the flames"*, then *"This time, you hold the barn at Thorncroft"*, with the horse back in its stall (g3 F3_3).
- **RF's two are both soft.** Several burning roofs become one smoking roof (g2 F4_3), and a herbalist already walking with the company is met as a stranger (g3 F5_3).
- **The playtest's class is a job whose target the failure destroys.** It lives on F3_3, where the plan may stake the barn on the first job. TC and HP did so in 5 sagas, and 4 of those contradicted it afterwards, for 7 contradictions. RF's other two F3_3 plans drew a find job and a talk job.
- **The counts are small.** 2 of 27 against 6 of 27 gives Fisher p = 0.25. The drop is large only in relative terms, and complete only for the hard class.

- **S10 on draws 1–2, where the prompts are identical:** +0.01 [−0.27, +0.29], so S10 itself is calm.
- **Most RF slice losses are plan-level.** The betrothal token never set up, a culprit with no discovery, a thin motive. These show up in TC too.
- **Three signs point at part (b):**
  - **Retry cards got barer:** 84 words against TC's 102, and three are 34–37 words. *"Last time, Douze, a frightened cooper, barred her door and would not listen. You could not win her over. Now you return to Brammarch to try again. Win her trust this time."*
  - **One retry card with no why invented an asker:** *"Now Rautio, a hunter, wants it found again"* (Rautio is the villain). The readers noticed: *"which contradicts the story"*.
  - **Failure and retry now repeat each other:** *"Two failed barn guards repeat each other almost word for word."* TC draws this comment too, but less often.
- **Part (a) has a stamp to watch (ledger 6b).** RF's failure reports say "stayed hidden" or "was still…" 7 times against TC's 1: *"The camp stayed hidden."*, *"The barn was still in danger."*

### Verdict

- **Part (a)** fixes the playtest's defect at the input. There are no hard contradictions in 27 failures, and the follow pairs on its slice are not worse.
- **Part (b)** is redundant once the target stands. The plan's framing ("hold the barn") is only false when the failure destroyed the barn, and (a) prevents that. (b) is also the likely source of the slice's S10 dip.
- (a) was never generated on its own, and the slice's S10 leans negative.
- **So: HOLD (a) for one cheap run (§6), and do not ship (b).** If the run confirms, (a) goes to both pipes, because the failure-report path is shared. The game default would become TC + stands and PP + stands.

## 5. Defects the verifier found in the shared pipeline (not fixed, they would change the default)

- **The cost goes to the wrong owner.** The engine sends cost as `{what, how, whose}` atoms, and the writer gave the company's lamed horse to the enemy (*"Benjamund's horse went down at the barrier and was lamed"*). This is a real wrong-owner bug in the game default. The lab's `clean` already has a fix (`ownCost`: one phrase naming the owner). It qualifies as a standalone defect fix on its own evidence; the designer's call.
- **The "needs you" opening.** The card-1 gloss *"who: the one who needs you"* is copied as the first sentence. 57 of 63 TC first cards (and 62 of 63 HP) contain "needs you". This is a shared stamp.
- The others (settles using gains, `edge` as "few words", the teller's "built from known", the four-clause `why`) touch slices too small for 72 pairs, or are already covered by `clean`, which S3 measured as neutral.

## 6. Recommendation

1. **Keep the game default:** PP on personal sagas, TC on the rest. Drop line (S1) and HP as arms.
2. **Decide RF (a) with one cheap run.** Generate RF without part (b) on the 8 draw-3 slots × 3 generations (about $1 per generation; draws 1–2 would equal TC). Then run the blind retry-contradiction count and one S10 batch: RF(a), TC and G0 draw-3 sagas, shuffled.
   - If hard contradictions stay at about 0 and S10 is not below TC beyond its CI, **ship (a) on both pipes:** TC → `voice` + stands, PP → `past` + stands. PP + stands is untested, but the failure report is the same template; if in doubt, add one pers1 draw with failures to the same run.
3. **Plateau: save and go bigger.** Tag fa86555 as the plateau state. The next change is the designer's own ruling, which is also this round's principle:
   - **Build the recurring personal arc** (North Star 0; STORY_ENGINE §46–57, §111): the bounded living dossier and chain B, a new matter grown from who the soldier became. It is the one place where a person's own past is already dealt, and where PP (+0.70 S10) showed the lever.
   - **For hired sagas, deal the past instead of asking for it.** One arm should hand the asker a past as an item, not as a prompt request: atoms that combine (a done deed, a person, an occasion), or a returning asker's memory of the company. Test it against TC with S10 inside one batch, against two incumbent draws.
4. **Designer's call, independent of the arms:** the cost-owner fix (§5).
