**Evidence for the structural classes:**

- **B, the part tag.** The names filter still sends `{"name":"Secile","label":"Short hot-headed sailor","part":"stands in the way"}` to every card whose `latest` mentions "sailor". This happens even when she is not in the job (F7_1, card 3).
  - Sonnet follows "Use only the data" literally and prints "Secile stands in the way."
  - The report for that job is not given her in `people`, so Sonnet invents a role for her: "Secile, a young woodsman… him". That is a sex flip and a J2 continuity error.
  - **All 4 of Sonnet's remaining "who is in the way" misses are part-tag cards** (F1_2 c2, F7_1 c2 and c3, F8_1 c1).
- **B, the ending menu.** Item 4 of the finale card prompt is "The choice, in one sentence", and the payload hands it the three fate strings. Both models recite them.
- **C, the answer line.** The plan says the answer is "hidden until the showdown" and `why` is "never the answer". The report prompt says "truth: one sentence … after leaves it out".
  - So nothing in jobs 1 to N seeds the answer, and the answer is always a separate line printed after the ending.
  - Sonnet writes better answers (M9, J1 "answered: yes"), but the delivery is the same.
  - Middle wins stay optional because of "the showdown works even if earlier jobs failed". Examples: F6_3's stolen deed, F8_3's bond-paper, F4_2's key.
- **D, continuity.** Memory is one ≤25-word summary per job. The `places` list "may repeat". Nothing tracks who is held or what was taken. Retries are not marked to the card writer: F3_3 tracks again a camp report 1 already found, and F4_3's retry card says the client is "new to you".
- **A, spoilers.** `why` ("how this job brings them closer") states the win in advance. Examples: F1_1 card 1 "His blind shows someone moves the stone by hand"; F6_2 card 2 "It proves the child fronts for a … merchant". Card-1 spoilers barely move (10 → 8).
- **A, paperwork.** The sneak type ("something is taken unseen") combined with a `why` that points toward proof makes about 85% of sneak jobs paperwork in both models. Sonnet's overall rate falls only because it picked fewer sneak jobs (17 vs 21) and more fights and hunts.
- **New gaps that the cheaper model would hit:**
  - Sonnet's plan gave the label "Clumsy", a trait rather than "words ending in a trade". The label lint passed it, and it printed as a name ("Clumsy's fate") in F3_2. That is the one J2 unmet-name leak.
  - The F6_1 plan answer names "Corvin", who is not in the cast. Sonnet's report dropped the name silently; gpt-5-mini would likely paste it. No lint checks names in the answer.
  - Plan logic: F8_2's finale has the company defend the drum that the client wants silenced.
