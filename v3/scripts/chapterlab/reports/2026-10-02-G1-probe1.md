## 7. The one revision the stop rule allows

Each item fixes a class, using the cheapest lever first.

1. **Card 1 moves to the `first` card call; the pitch is dropped from the plan prompt.** This is input shaping: the writer of card 1 never sees the answer, and the job arrives as the one imperative.
   - It removes three classes measured above: card-1 spoilers, job 1 told in the past tense, and the "who hires you" blur.
   - It also shortens the plan prompt.
2. **The finale report drops the dealt answer** (L 4 of 16; e.g. F3_3 below). The answer must get a fixed place in the output, either at the end of `after` or as its own field, not one data line among option, hurt and result.
3. **Paperwork in L.** The plan turns objects into ledgers, writs and deeds. The lever is a dealt mandate per job type, never adding more rule text. Keep the §2.7 lint as telemetry only.
4. **Continuity, the top classes from J2's notes:**
   - the finale report narrates an option that was not chosen ("Malene chose the bargain" when g0 was picked);
   - objects and places jump between hands and scenes;
   - people change sex;
   - an early report resolves a later job (F8_1: the wolfkin is returned in report 1, then card 2 asks for his rescue).
5. **Watch only:** the opener "You stand in/at…" starts 50–60% of card 1s, and the "a single <object> <verbs>" closer repeats. Card openers sharing a trigram: 37–47%, against base 7.6%.
