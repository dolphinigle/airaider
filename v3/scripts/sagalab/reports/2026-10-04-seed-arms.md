# Seed arms (seed1): five ways to seed the same 24 sagas, read blind. Sonnet writer, 2026-10-04

**Run.** `runs/seed1/{A0..A4}/<slot>/`: 24 slots × 5 arms, played through the game's own storyteller (`src/game/sagaflow.ts`) with the shipped prompts. Every arm had the same soldiers, dice and paths. Writer: Sonnet.

**Judging.** J3 whole-saga pairs, each slot read in both orders: 7 comparisons × 48 reads = 336 reads.

**Rule (North Star 0).** Readability first: **follow decides; keep is only watched.**

| arm | what the plan is given as its seed |
|---|---|
| **A0** themes (shipped) | one theme from `themes.json`. Cast: the client and the opponent, nobody else |
| **A1** kit | one situation + 1–3 random keyword atoms. Cast: client, opponent, 0–2 supporting people with no part |
| **A2** kit+pick | one situation + 10–11 keywords. A small pick call keeps the ones that fit (it kept exactly 3 every time) |
| **A3** kit+pick+situation | as A2, but 3 situations are offered and the pick also chooses one |
| **A4** kit+pick+premise | A2's deal and pick, then a premise call writes 3 sentences, which become the plan's seed |

## 0. Verdict

- **Ship A2 (kit+pick).** It is the only arm that wins follow against every other arm, and it wins in both display positions each time:

  | A2 against | all reads | held in both orders |
  |---|---|---|
  | A0 | 29–19 | 12–7 |
  | A1 | 29–19 | 13–8 |
  | A3 | 30–18 | 14–8 |
  | A4 | 32–16 | 16–8 |

- **No single comparison is significant.** The held-slot sign test gives p = 0.15–0.38. A2 is worth shipping because:
  - all four comparisons point the same way;
  - the designer has already ruled the theme library out (North Star 7), so the kit only needs to be no worse than A0, and on follow it is better.
- **Keep (watched): A0 still wins keep against every kit arm** (A2 loses it 19–29, held 7–12).
  - A theme arrives with a ready-made strange hook: "an alley the watch never finds", "bodies in herring barrels".
  - The kit deals plain nouns, so the plan has to invent the hook, and often doesn't.
- **The designer's question: the two-pass pick beats purely random keywords.**
  - Follow: 29–19, held 13–8.
  - Keep: a tie, 23–25.
- **The two extra steps don't help:**
  - **A3's situation pick buys interest and costs followability.** Against A2: keep 27–21, follow 18–30.
  - **A4's premise step hurts both.** It loses follow to A0 by the widest margin of any arm (15–33, held 6–15, p ≈ 0.08). It also loses to A2 on the very same deals (16–32, held 8–16, no slot flipped).
- **New failure classes the kit brings:**
  - **keywords forced in:** the pick always keeps 3, and the plan is told to "use each";
  - **supporting people as errands or second villains;**
  - **(A4 only) the premise has the client hire someone from the cast, often the very person the ending decides,** so that person turns from ally to foe mid-saga.

## 2. The designer's question: random keywords (A1) or the two-pass pick (A2)?

**The pick produces sagas that are easier to follow, and they are no less interesting.**
- Head to head, A2 beats A1 on follow 29–19 (held 13–8, 3 flips, p ≈ 0.38).
- Against the shipped A0, A2 wins follow (29–19) and A1 loses it (21–27).
- Keep is a tie between them (23–25, held 9–10), and both lose keep to A0.

Why, from the plans and the readers' reasons:

- **With only 1–3 random atoms, the plan fills the jobs with the supporting cast.**
  - A1 put 25 of its 28 supporting people on stage; A2 used 20 of 29.
  - Readers blame A1 for this:
    - "Y piles up three elves (a moneylender, a baron, a slaver who appears out of nowhere)" (F2_2);
    - "X's still and shepherd jobs felt like errands" (F1_1);
    - "drops in a silent tax collector" (F2_3).
- **A lone atom becomes the object the story is about, and that works.** The pie became a missing pie master; the brooch and the necklace became the thing the story follows; the raven became a clue that comes back in the finale.
- **An odd pair of atoms becomes a detour job.**
  - Shield + kraken: "why does tracking a kraken win the fowler's trust?" (F6_3).
  - Falcon + kraken: "why hunt a kraken to free a debtor?" (F2_3).
- **The pick has a failure of its own: it always keeps 3.** It kept exactly 3 in all 72 picks, though the prompt says "one to three", and the plan prompt says "keywords: use each". A misfit atom then gets forced in:
  - an eclipse in a mine-debt story: "an eclipse shows up out of nowhere in the finale" (A2 F8_1, both orders);
  - a horse race: "never explains why wrecking a fisher's boat would save a horse-race bet" (A2 F6_3).
- **Cost and speed.** The pick call costs about $0.003 and takes 3.4 s (p50). Card 1 arrives at 47.6 s (p50), against 43.9 s for A1 and 41.8 s for A0. A2 costs $0.1025 per saga against A0's $0.0976 (Sonnet list price).

## 3. The best arm against A0, and what A3 and A4 add over A2

**Best against A0: A2.**
- On follow, A2 and A3 score almost the same against A0 (29–19 and 28–20).
- Head to head, A2 beats A3 on follow 30–18 (held 14–8), winning 15 of 24 reads in each position.
- Against A0, A2 and A3 have the same number of held follow wins (12 each).

**A3 (the pick also chooses the situation) adds interest and costs followability.**
- It is the best kit arm on keep, both against A0 (21–27) and against A2 (27–21, held 13–10; it wins in both positions, but the result is not significant).
- **Where the interest comes from: the pick steers toward a lost thing or a hidden truth.**
  - It moved off the dealt situation in 16 of 21 non-personal slots.
  - It took "recover something lost" and "get back something stolen" every time they were offered (5 of 5), and "expose a lie" 2 of 3.
  - It also took "end a haunting" and "stop a wedding" (each offered once), "get rid of a beast" and "lift a curse" (1 of 2 each).
  - It never chose "guard something precious", "rescue someone" or "drive someone out" (0 of 4 each), nor "free a prisoner" or "win someone over" (0 of 3 each).
  - These situations produce the hooks readers want: "heavy with milk, yet no cub" (F4_3); "a worn saddle that 'clinks'" (F7_3); the wyvern's "knotted jess" (F1_3).
- **Where the follow loss comes from:**
  - When the situation is picked rather than dealt, the client's reason can go missing: "Rosrytta's reason to stop the wedding is never given" (F4_1).
  - Extra people get seated as filler: "the hedge-knight roadblock 'for coin' felt like filler" (F1_1).

**A4 (a premise before the plan) makes both measures worse.**
- **Readability.** A4 loses follow to A0 by the widest margin (15–33; A1, the other loser, is 21–27). On A2's own deals it loses 16–32 (held 8–16, no flips).
- **Interest.** It also loses keep to A0 (15–33). It runs about even with A2 on keep (22–26).
- **The mechanism** (details in §4, N4): the premise writes three hooks before the plan has chosen an answer or any jobs, and the plan has to honour all three.

## 4. The reasons, grouped by class

The counts are hand tallies of the follow reasons, approximate.

### Follow: what readers credit
- **One thread, one object.**
  - "X follows one object, the necklace" (A1 F1_3).
  - "One clean chain: handwriting, then 'writing in her books', then 'he kept her secret books and would speak'" (A2 F2_3).
  - "Y follows one object from the inn to the drover, the goatherd and the sailor" (A3 F7_3).
- **Each job hands the next one its tool.**
  - "each step hands the next its tool: the map, then the stick, then the confrontation" (A0 F2_2).
  - "Ledger, then courier, then counting house: every step points to the next" (A2 F2_3).
- **The goal is plain on card 1, and one opponent runs all the way through.**
  - "Y's goal is plain from line one: get the stolen lantern back from a known thief" (A0 F7_3).
  - "X has one villain, the steward, from start to end" (A2 F2_2).
- **The finale says the answer plainly and uses what the company holds.**
  - "every held item comes back in the finale: the scout names the hour and the herbs clear the fog" (A4 F5_3).

### Follow: what readers blame (every arm, A0 included)
1. **Set up only in the finale, or out of nowhere.** The most common class in every arm.
   - "an eclipse shows up out of nowhere in the finale" (A2 F8_1).
   - "card 3 suddenly says 'before he sells the warhorse a third time'" (A2 F3_3).
   - "the 'bloodstained birdcage', the 'warden's courier' and 'the weaver's tale' all show up for the first time in the finale" (A4 F3_1).
   - "the painting is first mentioned in a finale plan" (A1 F3_3).
   - "answers a beast question ('No beast had ever stalked this forest') that no card ever asked" (A0 F1_1).
2. **Contradictions within the saga.** Writer continuity, in all arms.
   - "The hunter hands over the pouch in report 1, then the finale finds that same pouch in a chest" (A2 F1_2).
   - "report 1 says the ram is gone, then card 2 says 'the ram stays in its pen'" (A1 F3_1).
   - "Marsilia is caught and bound in report 1, then turns up running the mint" (A0 F5_2).
3. **Who is on whose side.**
   - "Rautio starts as a helper and turns into the warden's killer without warning" (A4 F1_3).
   - "Secile is a passenger ally in part 1, then lands with her own crew as the villain" (A4 F7_2).
   - "Phillipos lifts the toll, then two lines later 'stands against you'" (A2 F3_2).
   - A0's own: "I couldn't tell whether Muvulrea was the rival or just a guide" (A0 F4_2). This cost A0 all eight of its follow reads in that slot.
4. **Too many people, or people from nowhere.** Mostly a kit-arm class.
   - "three elves" (A1 F2_2).
   - "the herbalist Kordula appears from nowhere" (A4 F5_3).
   - "a guild master, Uurkar, nobody introduced" (A3 F8_2).
   - "the cook pays Benjamund, but 'Known' says a trader pays the raiders" (A2 F6_1).
5. **Detour jobs.**
   - "'learn where the pike lurks, then follow it' makes no sense" (A2 F4_3).
   - The kraken twice (A1).
   - "its bear and goatherd jobs barely touch the rod" (A4 F7_2).
   - A0's own: "its boar hunt has no link to either" (A0 F7_3).
6. **A muddled goal or an odd question.**
   - A0, where the theme cannot seat the dealt cast:
     - "the odd night-with-his-wife goal took rereading" (F5_1);
     - "it's unclear what Laudus is accused of" (F5_3);
     - "says the goal is 'deadlock' when the real problem is that votes don't bind" (F1_3).

     This is the designer's item-7 complaint, confirmed by blind readers.
   - A4:
     - "asks why 'the elf wanderer's fate hangs on this debt'" (F4_1);
     - "'who made the kite' is a strange question to open on" (F8_2).
7. **Name and pronoun slips.**
   - "switches the weaver from 'her' to 'his'" (A3 F2_1).
   - "Benjamin and Benjamund" (A3 F6_1).
   - "the steward switches from she to he" (A0 F4_2).

**A2's 19 follow losses to A0, by class:** about 8 set up only in the finale, about 5 contradictions, 2 detours, 2 "who pays whom", 1 side switch, 1 unclear charge. Four of the 19 are the forced keywords eclipse and horse race, two reads each.

**A4's 33 losses** are dominated by the premise: side switches (about 10 reads) and premise hooks paid off only at the end or never.

### Keep (watched)
- **Credited:**
  - **A concrete odd clue:**
    - "biting a coin and finding soft lead under the skin" (A0 F5_2);
    - "a crate that hummed" (A0 F7_3);
    - "heavy with milk, yet no cub" (A3 F4_3);
    - "bread and candles ('she feeds someone hidden')" (A2 F4_2).
  - **A personal stake:** a missing son, daughter or sister; a child; a mother facing the rope.
  - **A twist that changes the motive:**
    - "he chose the bond to protect his sister" (A0 F2_3);
    - "the roots she's felling are what hold the flood back" (A2 F8_2);
    - "she gnawed the oar to survive" (A4 F7_3).
  - **A setup paid off in the finale:**
    - "the tame hare taken in part 1 lures him off the stair" (A2 F1_3);
    - "setting the trapped gnawer on the dam" (A2 F7_3).
- **Blamed:**
  - **Low stakes:** "a fishing rod", "a spoiled wine cask before a feast", "sheep at a toll gate feels small", a stolen kite.
  - **An answer guessable early:** "rotten oak is the obvious answer after card 1".
  - **A flat payoff:** "forgot which chest", "a drunk sale he forgot".
- **Why A0 keeps its keep lead.** A0's theme is itself the odd clue: "'The forest road has grown longer' is an eerie hook"; "a stranger who washed ashore wearing a fisherman's name". A0's F8_3 (bodies in herring barrels) won keep in all 8 of its reads.

### New failure classes the kit arms bring
- **N1. A keyword forced in.** The pick keeps 3 every time, and the plan is told to "use each", so an atom that doesn't belong becomes a deadline or a detour with no reason.
  - eclipse (A2 and A4 F8_1);
  - kraken (A1 F2_3, F6_3);
  - horse race (A2 F6_3);
  - nightingale (A4 F6_3).
- **N2. Supporting people turned into errands or second villains.**
  - It scales with how many the plan seats: A1 seated 25/28, A4 23/29, A3 22/29, A2 20/29.
  - Dropping them costs nothing a reader can see. A2 left 9 dealt people off stage.
  - They help only when the person carries one clue on the main thread:
    - A2 F1_1: the potter reads the coin mark;
    - A4 F4_3: the fisher rows the supplies.
- **N3. A dealt trade that copies the company.** "Mercenary captain" sits in the supporting-trade pool (F8_2, F8_3), so two of A4's premises have the client hire *him*.
- **N4. The premise over-commits (A4).** Its three sentences have three failure modes:
  - **It echoes engine wording.** The premise prompt describes the person the ending decides as "the end decides their fate".
    - 17 of 24 premises repeat this as "…and his fate hangs on it".
    - Twice it reached the question and card 1 word for word: "Nobody yet knows why the elf wanderer's fate hangs on this debt" (F4_1); "why the human sailor's fate hangs on reaching the island" (F7_2).
  - **It seats a cast member as the client's hired help.** The prompt says "use only the cast" and never says the company is the help.
    - Five premises have the client hire someone from the cast: "hires a hunter and a beekeeper" (F1_3), "hires an elf wanderer" (F4_1), "asks a sailor for hired help" (F7_1), "hires the wolfkin mercenary captain" (F8_2, F8_3).
    - Two more seat the sailor as transport or as someone to win over (F7_2, F7_3).
    - When the hired one is the person the ending decides (F1_3, F4_1, F7_1, and the sailor in F7_2 and F7_3), the plan still seats them as the opponent, so they switch from ally to foe.
    - A4 lost follow to A0 in both orders in 6 of these 7 slots.
  - **Its "nobody knows" sentence plants a secret the plan must honour, and the plan reveals it only at the end:**
    - "the brother's betrothal contract" → "a 'betrothal contract' turns up from nowhere" (F2_1);
    - the birdcage and the courier (F3_1);
    - "she wants Jervaise's company sword" → "'wants your company's sword more than any coin' is vague" (F2_2);
    - "a caged nightingale whose song hides the race's true prize" → "fuzzy from the start" (F6_3).
  - **What this teaches:** a pre-plan text step that writes hooks without an answer breaks the answer-first rule (ledger 6d). The plan then has to find an answer that fits hooks it didn't choose.
- **N5. The client's reason goes missing when the situation is picked (A3).** "Stop a wedding" was picked, and nobody said why the client wants it stopped (F4_1).
- **N6. The pick is noisy.** On identical deals A2 and A4 picked the same 3 keywords in only 15 of 24 slots.

## 5. What the dealt inputs did: six slots read from plan.json

**F1_1: one slot under all five arms.**
- **A0** (theme "a lord has ordered the village wall built from its own houses"; grim; two people).
  - The plan invented the hunter's scheme: a faked beast, and tithe silver under the floors.
  - Strong hooks: A0 won keep in all 8 reads.
  - But report 2 debunks a beast no card had mentioned, and A0 lost follow to A1, A2 and A4 in both orders.
- **A1** ("guard something precious" + eclipse; brewer and shepherd added).
  - The eclipse became the deadline for guarding a "Moonseed".
  - Each supporting person got a job of their own (the brewer's still, winning over the shepherd), and readers called them "errands".
- **A2** (same situation; 10 offered, picked bees, farm, harvest; potter added).
  - One plain question: "Nobody knows why the hunter keeps coming back to wreck Eate's hives when he never takes any honey."
  - Three object clues: the muddy spade, coins green with age, a clay tally with the coin mark.
  - The potter is used once, as the witness who reads the mark.
  - **One clear story.** It won follow in both orders against A0, A1, A3 and A4.
- **A3** (picked "get back something stolen" + pig, orchard, helmet; hedge knight and weaver added).
  - "The helmet was the lid" is a good hook.
  - But the hedge knight is seated as a fight with no reason, and one line is garbled ("Puyatha's weaver caught his scent"). It lost follow to A0 and to A2.
- **A4** (A2's deal).
  - The premise added "a lizardfolk potter needs the wax" and "the hunter's choice at harvest decides his own fate".
  - The plan hid a purse in a stump. Easy to follow (it beat A0 in both orders) but flat: "every clue just points back to 'the stump'". It lost follow to A2 in both orders.

**F4_2: atoms that became clues (A2) against a premise that invented a villain (A4).**
- **A2** ("find someone who is missing" + gallows, crow, candle; grim; no supporting people dealt).
  - Every atom became something the company finds: a trail of tied crow feathers, a candle mark beside a struck-out hanging, and the gallows night as the question.
  - It is shown in full in §7.
- **A4** (same picks).
  - The premise invented "a crow-marked killer" and a missing brother, and the plan made the wanderer the killer.
  - It is A4's best saga: it beat A0 in both orders on both measures. But it lost both measures to A2 in both orders.
- **A3** moved the situation to "end a haunting" (gallows, standing stones, music box): "the music box gives away the haunting in step 1".

**F8_1: one forced atom.**
- **A2** ("collect what is owed" + mine, well, eclipse; toll-keeper and mason both used well).
  - The eclipse got no job and no clue. It surfaces only in the finale: "stop Natalgreesh at the Marlwell well before the eclipse".
  - The plan's answer contradicts its own ending. The answer says she "poured it into secretly sealing the Marlwell well"; the ending says "The silver owed to Autonoe's village is paid in full from the merchant's strongbox."
  - A0 won follow in both orders.
- **A4** (same deal, but the pick took lighthouse, mine, eclipse).
  - The premise wrote "the merchant will die at the eclipse".
  - The plan ignored that sentence and built a fraud around the lighthouse: shares sold in a dead vein, and the dam broken on purpose.
  - It won follow and keep over both A0 and A2, in both orders.
  - So a plan can drop a premise line, and here that was the good outcome.

**F2_2: the same inputs, three different results (a personal saga).**
- The soldier's past is the seed in every arm. A2, A3 and A4 got the same cast (a farmer and a horse dealer), the same tone and the same picked keywords (ford, duel, dye works).
- **A2:** the answer is that the brother "alone knows the dye secret". Each supporting person brings one clue: the farmer, the ledger marking the bond paid; the horse dealer, the note on the vats. It won follow and keep in all 16 of its reads.
- **A3** (identical inputs) reached nearly the same answer but lost both measures to A2 in both orders ("Roruld 'saw something in the vats' that was never set up"). That is generation noise alone.
- **A4:** the premise turned "duel" into a steward who "refuses payment, demanding a duel at the ford, because she wants Jervaise's company sword". Readers: "'wants your company's sword more than any coin' is vague".
- **A1** dealt one atom (manor) and two elf supporting people (moneylender, cattle baron) next to an elf slaver: "three elves" piled up.

**F6_3: four seeds, one story that worked.**
- **A1** (shield, kraken): the kraken hunt is a detour.
- **A2** (horse race, island, peacock): the plan chained all three (bets on one horse → false lights wreck the rival's boat → a chest holding only a saddle and silks), but readers never saw the link.
- **A3** moved the situation to "find someone who is missing", with cave, fog and island.
  - The trail is plain: the reeve read the tallies, her own tracks walk off under a heavy pack, the pilot was paid to keep quiet.
  - It won follow over A0 and A2 in both orders, and keep over A2.
- **A4**'s premise added "a caged nightingale whose song hides the race's true prize": "fuzzy from the start".

**F3_3: people dealt but never met.**
- **A2** ("stop an attack" + farm, barn, warhorse) is a short saga (N = 2, last-chance path).
- Of the two dealt supporting people, the goatherd never appears. The magistrate appears only inside the reveal ("He had sold the horse to Eraldil and to the magistrate, and the raiders were the magistrate's men"), and readers praised that reveal ("X's double sale explains everything").
- The follow complaint is the plan's own line: "before he sells the warhorse a third time", a sale nobody had seen.

**What these six show:**
- **One clear story comes most often from A2:** a picked set of 3 atoms that the plan turns into objects the company finds.
- **It breaks in three ways:**
  - one atom that doesn't fit gets forced in (eclipse, horse race);
  - the supporting people get seated as errands;
  - a premise step pre-commits the plan.
- **Supporting people help only when one of them carries a clue on the main thread** (the potter, the farmer, the fisher). Otherwise readers notice them only as noise.

## 6. Recommendation

1. **Ship `kit+pick`.** It is one default change: `SEED_ARM` in `v3/src/engine/saga.ts`, from `'themes'` to `'kit+pick'`. It is the readability winner, and the designer has already ruled the themes out. Keep loses to A0 (19–29); that is watched, and per North Star 0 not a reason to wait.
2. **Do not ship A3 or A4.** Keep A3 in mind as the interest lever: picking the situation steers stories toward mysteries.
3. **Content fix, no prompt change:** filter `mercenary captain` out of `SUPPORT_TRADES` (which spreads in `POWER_TRADES`). It can stay in `POWER_TRADES` for personal opponents. As a supporting person it reads as the company's double (N3).
4. **Ledger lines for North Star 9:**
   - **Worked:** kit+pick over themes on follow (29–19, held 12–7) and over random keywords (29–19, held 13–8).
   - **Did not:** a premise call before the plan. It costs follow (16–32 against kit+pick on the same deals) because it writes hooks with no answer, echoes engine wording ("fate"), and seats a cast member as hired help.
   - **Did not:** a situation pick. It costs follow (18–30); it buys keep (27–21).
5. **The next input-pipeline experiment (item 8): "deal plenty, keep few" applied to people.**
