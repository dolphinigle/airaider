# Round D (seed1): two one-change arms on C2, 3 generations each, read blind · plus C2 out of sample (seed2). Sonnet writer, 2026-10-04

**Run.**
- seed1: `runs/seed1/{D1,D2}_g{1,2,3}/<slot>/` (24 slots each, 144 sagas). All 144 completed on the first pass.
  - Every saga plays A2's dealt world (cast, offered keywords, tone, places), so it gets the same deal as C2's three draws. Soldiers, dice and outcome paths match C2 in 144 of 144.
  - The keyword pick is an AI call, so a D saga and the C2 draw it was read against kept different keywords in 3–8 of 24 slots per generation.
- seed2: `runs/seed2/{A2,C2}_g{1,2}/` (draws 4–6, 96 sagas). All completed on the first pass. Every slot replays A2_g1's deal (`_deals/`), and outcomes match across all four runs.
  - The pick differed in 4 of the 12 seed2 pairs that were read.
- Spend (seed1, per saga at list price): C2 $0.096, D1 $0.099, D2 $0.099. Card 1 median: about 45 s in all three. seed2: A2 $0.105, C2 $0.098.

**Judging.** J3 whole-saga pairs, both orders. D*g* was read against C2g* (C2's three draws from the pipeline round are the incumbent). That gives 72 slot-pairs and 144 reads per arm. seed2 C2g* was read against A2g*. 312 reads in all.

**Rule (North Star 0).** Readability first: **follow decides; keep is watched.**

| arm | the one change on top of C2 (grafts) |
|---|---|
| **D1** reads | After a won middle job, the next card's `latest` is the report's one-sentence summary, the text the player just read, instead of the plan's forecast `win`. No prompt change. |
| **D2** fixes | (a) The report's hope line is narrowed: "show only what result and clue leave unmet; add no event". (b) Every finale card gets the stake (`lose`) beside the trouble's `will`. (c) The engine joins the fate and settles into one result sentence, and the secret is told "in your own words". |

**Noise floor.** Two identical arms over three generations land within **±15 held** pairs 95% of the time. One more calibration from this round: D1, D2 and C2 share the plan prompt, so plan-level class shares (set up nowhere, side swaps, detours, reveals) should match. Here they differ by up to 15 points at these sample sizes. So **class shares are noise below about ±15 points.**

## 0. Verdict

- **Keep C2 alone as the game default.** Neither D arm beat it. **Combining them is not supported.**
- **D1 (the card reads the report): no gain.**
  - **Follow: 61–83, held 23–34** (p ≈ 0.19). This is inside the floor. D1 was behind in generations 1 and 3 and ahead by one held pair in generation 2. Slot majorities: 5–12, with 7 ties.
  - **Keep: flat**, 73–71 (held 27–26).
  - **Contradictions did not fall.** They are in 36% of D1's follow losses, against 34% of C2's in the same pairs.
  - In the texts, D1 fixed its own mechanism: 135 of 141 cards after a won job now open on the company as the doer, against 111 for C2, with no "Someone took…" openers. But readers had blamed that mechanism in only about 3 of C2's 125 losses.
- **D2 (report and finale fixes): no gain, and a likely keep cost.**
  - **Follow: 64–80, held 23–31** (p ≈ 0.34). D2 was behind in all three generations, by 6, 1 and 1 held pairs.
  - **Keep: 57–87, held 19–34** (p ≈ 0.053). It was behind in all three generations. That is −15 held, right at the floor's edge.
  - **The stake became a stock sentence.** "If you fail, X loses Y." closes 71 of 72 D2 finale cards, against 3 of 72 for C2. This is ledger law 6(b) again: every field the writer gets as its own item becomes its own stock sentence.
  - Its narrowed hope line did work in the texts: won-job summaries carrying a shortfall fell from 17 to 5 of 192.
- **seed2: C2 beats shipped kit+pick on new slots, but only half of them were read.**
  - The judging pass read 12 of 24 slots, one generation each (§2).
  - **Follow: 16–8, held 7–3** (p ≈ 0.34). **Keep: 21–3, held 9–0** (p ≈ 0.004).
  - **Detours replicate.** They are in 10 of kit+pick's 16 follow losses and 1 of C2's 8; on seed1 it was 20% against 5%.
  - The keep win replicates out of sample. Follow points the same way, but 12 slots cannot measure it.
- **Where the contradictions really come from (§3.1).** It is not the card's `latest`.
  - About a third of all contradiction reads are a later card restating the plan's **pre-play showdown**: its job, its trouble's `will`, or its stake. The plan's own earlier gains and learns already overturned it ("take the ring from her" while the ring is on the corpse).
  - The rest are place drift, pronoun drift and report logic.
  - The plan writes the showdown as if the jobs before it had not happened.
- **Plateau watch (North Star 4).** Round D is the first round since C2 to fail to beat the best checkpoint. If the next round also fails, the plateau rule applies.
  - The big follow-loss classes (set up nowhere, about 40%; reveals; detours; side swaps) come from the plan, which C2, D1 and D2 all share. Downstream input fixes cannot reach them, so the next arm should work on the plan (§4).
- **Three defects in the shipped default, to fix as bugs** (they need no arm; details in §3.3):
  - a doubled "hopes" in road rows: 3 sagas;
  - a garbled label for clients with bynames ("an of the human woman"): 3 sagas, 5 reads;
  - gold-way money pasted into the story: 16–17 of 18 gold finales in every graft arm, against 0 for A2. The designer has already ruled that it goes on the button only, and that change is being made in the working tree.
- **Harness defect.** The seed2 judging pass asked for seed1 slot names, read only 12 slots, and its tally reported held 0–0 because the two orders carried different slot labels. I re-paired every read by its real seed2 slot by hand (§2).

### 3.1 Contradiction anatomy: the plan contradicts itself at the finale

- **The size.** Of about 100 contradiction reads across the four loss columns, **36** blame a later card, usually the finale card, for stating something the earlier texts had already overturned.
- **The pattern.** In every case I checked, the overturned fact is in the plan itself:
  - the showdown's `job`, `trouble.will` or `lose` contradicts a `gain` or `learn` the same plan gave an earlier job;
  - the writer pastes both.
- **The cases I checked in `plan.json`:**

| saga | earlier job (plan) | showdown (same plan) | reader |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 g2 F4_1 | gain *the coffin*; learn *"…with the ring still on his finger"* | job *"…take the ring from her"*; carries *"a longbow and the ring"* | "the ring is on the corpse in report 1, but card 3 says the wanderer carries it" |
| D2 g1 F1_1 | ep 3 gain *a jar of harvest honey* | lose *"his hives and his harvest"* | "the finale card warns of losing a harvest already gathered" |
| C2 g3 F5_1 | ep 1 gain *the seized hide cart* | job *"…take back the hides"* | "the finale has her barricaded in a kiln 'to take back the hides' they already hold" |
| C2 g3 F3_1 | ep 1: the company holds the gallery and the painting | job *"Catch the merchant before he takes the painting…"* | "Urreturre holds the painting, yet the merchant is 'taking the painting from the town'" |
| D2 g1 F7_2 | ep 3 gain *the sailor's hidden skiff*; learn: the rod is wrapped in oilcloth | job *"…take back the rod"* | "report 3 finds the oilcloth-wrapped rod in the skiff, but in the finale Secile holds the rod" |

- **Why D1 could not reach it.** D1 changes `latest`. These contradictions sit in `job`, `trouble` and `lose`, which the card gets as finished sentences.
  - The card is also never given the Known or Held lines the engine prints above it. So it cannot see that the ring is already in hand.
  - This is the same law as R7's "engine ledger lines the writer never saw", applied here to the card.
- **The other contradictions** are writer drift and report logic:
  - places swapped between cards (Hawlea/Fallea, Marlworth/Peatlea, Birmoss/Lingworth);
  - pronouns flipped (the reeve "she" then "he", the courier "his" then "her");
  - report-internal logic ("Ysausa freed herself past a barred door");
  - an engine cost atom against the prose ("lost a horse to the woodsmen's arrows" in the summary, "the horse bolted" in the report, so the next card says it was killed).

### 3.2 New and confirmed classes

- **Stock stake line (D2, confirmed law 6b).** "If you fail, X loses Y." ends 71 of 72 finale cards.
  - It is a forecast, so it can contradict play (the harvest case).
  - It may also flatten the close. D2's keep loss is consistent with that, but the three D2 changes were not separated.
- **Carried shortfalls (D1).** A won job's summary sometimes says what the job did *not* yet give ("…did not yet name him"). D1 hands that summary to the next card, which repeats it (8 cards against 0).
  - Readers then catch the finale contradicting the shortfall.
  - This is why D1 would need D2's hope fix underneath it. Neither one showed a gain, so the pairing is not worth an arm.
- **The ending contradicts the answer.** 7 reads, in 3 sagas, all in arms with C2's "the answer need not fit every ending":
  - C2 g2 F8_1: the answer says the silver went into the well's lining, then "she opens a strongbox and pays it anyway";
  - C2 g1 F8_3: the answer says the wine is spoiled, yet "Pirtar pours [the bitter wine] at the feast anyway";
  - D2 g1 F8_1: "her wealth was a bluff, yet silver is carried out".
  - This is the price of C2's change (d), which removed hoard answers 16 → 5. It is small, but it should be watched.
- **The soldier announces the secret.** About 6 reads across all arms. Examples:
  - "Trelon guesses a lot from very little";
  - "Khala simply declares the shard came from the true mirror";
  - "Eddusa only says it 'all made sense now'".
  - This is the context-free verifier's report-12 item ("'Someone' can be anyone, so the deciding soldier simply states the secret").
- **A printed hope is a promise** (the pipeline report's new small class). It held steady at 3–8% of losses. Examples:
  - "the kite's purpose (calling her kin) is never used";
  - "Y promises witnesses who never show up".

### 3.3 Engine defects in the shipped C2 (bugs, not arms)

1. **Doubled "hopes" in road rows.**
   - Example: "Donglanel Brightwater hopes that with the boat back in her hands, she hopes to give it to the fisher."

3. **Gold-way money in the story text.**
   - "…paid the company to go free" appears in 16–17 of 18 gold finales in C2, D1 and D2, and in 0 of 18 in A2.

### 4.3 Next experiment (item 8): E1, "the showdown from the jobs"

- **The change, on top of C2.**
  - The plan stops after the jobs before the finale. It still writes the question, the answer, the cast, the ways and each job's gain and learn.
  - A second small call writes the showdown's `job`, `trouble` and `lose`. It runs alongside card 1, so card 1 is not delayed.
  - Its input: the answer, every earlier job with its gain and learn, and the people those jobs meet.
  - Its output is checked against the same fields the plan's showdown has today. The card's prompt does not change.
- **Principle.** It is the converse of C1's law: the step that fixes the finale's facts sees everything that points at them, and it is written after them, not beside them.
  - This splits the work *downstream*, like card 1 as its own call, which worked.
- **Target:**
  - the forecast-contradiction class (§3.1: about a third of contradiction blame, 7–19% of follow losses);
  - finale foes who were never met. The call is handed the people the jobs meet.
- **Power, honestly.** That class is under the noise floor on its own, the same trap D1 fell into.
  - So count it mechanically as well: add a log-only lint for a showdown job, will or lose that names an earlier gain, and compare against C2's rate.
  - Judge 3 generations against C2's three draws. Count it a gain only if it is ahead in all three generations, its mechanical count falls, and keep does not fall.
  - A measured gain still needs a pooled held margin beyond ±15.
- **If E1 also fails, the plateau rule applies** (North Star 4): save the state and change how the plan works.
  - The candidate would be the top class itself, set up nowhere (about 40% of losses in every arm).
  - About half of those reads name a person first seen at the finale who is already in the plan's answer. For example: "Marz-Ja turns up out of nowhere in the finale"; "the tinker first appears on the finale card"; "a horse dealer appears out of nowhere in the finale".
  - The lever would be what the plan is handed about seating each supporting person before the finale, not a wording pass.
  - Caution: B1 measured that a person handed over as "chosen" lands in the question or answer. Any seating deal must not label people.
