# B1 (kit+pick+cast) against kit+pick, measured against a second generation of kit+pick (seed1). Sonnet writer, 2026-10-04

**Run.**
- Folders: `runs/seed1/{A2b,B1}/<slot>/`, 24 slots each. Every slot used A2's soldiers, dice and paths.
- **A2′** (folder `A2b`) is A2's 24 dealt worlds, read back from A2's `plan.json` (24 of 24 identical). The pick, the plan and every card were then generated a second time.
- **B1** is kit+pick+cast.
- 48 of 48 sagas completed on the first pass: no re-runs, no floor, no failed calls.

**Judging.** J3 whole-saga pairs, each slot read in both orders. Two comparisons: B1 vs A2 and A2′ vs A2. 96 reads.

**Rule (North Star 0).** Readability first: **follow decides; keep is only watched.**

| | A2 kit+pick (shipped) | B1 kit+pick+cast |
|---|---|---|
| supporting people | 0–2 dealt, all passed to the plan | 3–4 dealt; the pick keeps 0–1; the plan sees only that one |
| keywords to the plan | the pick keeps 1–3 (it always kept 3) | the pick lists them best first; the engine keeps the top 2 |
| plan prompt | — | unchanged |

**Confounds.**
- B1's trades come from the corrected lists: no mercenary captain, and no two supporting people share a trade.
- B1's places differ from A2's in the same slot, because the extra people are coined before the places.
- The client, situation, offered keywords and tone match A2.

## 0. Verdict

- **Keep `kit+pick` as shipped. Do not switch to B1.**
- **The noise floor is wide.** A2′ vs A2 is the same arm on the same deals, and it still splits: follow 22–26 (held 9–11), keep 28–20 (held 12–8). With 24 slots, one run cannot separate two arms that differ by less than about ±9 held slots (±18 reads).
- **B1 vs A2: follow 19–29** (held 9–14, 1 flip, p ≈ 0.41), lost in both display positions.
  - That is inside the floor, so it is not a measured loss.
  - It is no gain in any cut either.
  - Keep: 20–28 (held 7–11).
- **B1 removed neither failure class it was built to remove.**
  - **Keyword forced in:** still 6 of B1's 29 follow losses (21%). That is the same rate as A2's losses to A0 (4 of 19).
  - **Extra people as errands:** errands fell (supporting people with a job of their own, 17 → 11). A new class replaced them: **the picked person takes over the question.**
    - The kept person sits in the plan's question or answer in 15 of 21 sagas. In A2 it was 9 of 29.
    - 12 of B1's 29 follow losses name that person.
- **Next (item 8): B2 "one object".** It changes one input against A2: the engine keeps only the pick's top keyword. The cast and plan prompt stay as A2's. The run replays A2's deals and is judged against both A2 and A2′ (§4).

## 1. The noise floor: A2′ against A2

A2′ has A2's deals exactly; only the generation differs. The pick was generated again too, and it kept the same keywords in 14 of 24 slots. In those 14 slots, every input the plan received was identical to A2's.

| A2′ vs A2 | all reads | A2′ 1st / 2nd | held (flipped) | p (held) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| follow | 22–26 | 11 / 11 | 9–11 (4) | 0.82 |
| keep | 28–20 | 13 / 15 | 12–8 (4) | 0.50 |

- **Identical inputs still produce confident winners.** In the 14 identical-input slots, follow went 5–6 held, with 3 flips. Generation alone produced a winner in both orders in 11 of 14. The readers are consistent; the writer is not.
  - So "held in both orders" means the reader reliably prefers that one draw. It does not mean the arm is better.
- **What one 24-slot run can resolve.** About 83% of slots hold in both orders. Two identical arms therefore land within about ±9 held slots (±18 reads, roughly 33–15) of a tie 95% of the time.
- **The incumbent's draw decides most slots.**
  - B1 vs A2 and A2′ vs A2 point the same way in 16 of 24 slots; only 3 point opposite ways.
  - 7 of B1's 9 held follow wins are slots where A2′ also beat A2. These are A2's weak draws:
    - the pouch that is handed over and later found in a chest (F1_2);
    - Phillipos switching sides (F3_2);
    - the unexplained "third" warhorse sale (F3_3);
    - the pike detour (F4_3);
    - the fox from nowhere (F5_2);
    - the wager that is never explained (F6_3);
    - the chest logic (F7_3).
  - 9 of B1's 14 held follow losses are slots where A2 also beat A2′. These are A2's strong draws: the crow at the gallows (F4_2), the flood dyke (F8_2), the fake-ale casks (F2_1), the storm-calling rod (F7_2), the two keys (F7_1).
- **The seed-arms results, read again with this floor:**
  - **A2's follow wins** (29–19 against A0, held 12–7; 29–19 against A1, held 13–8): each one alone is inside the floor. kit+pick still stands, because four comparisons point the same way and the designer had already ruled out the themes.
  - **A0's keep lead over A2** (29–19, held 12–7) is the same size as the keep swing from regenerating A2 (28–20). It is a direction, since all four kit arms lose keep to A0, but not a measured gap.
  - **A4's loss to A2** (16–32, held 8–16, no flips) is the only seed1 result near the edge of the floor.
- **Position bias.** Follow: the saga shown first won 24 of 48 reads, so there is none. Keep: the first saga won 22 of 48, the same slight lean to the second saga seen before.

## 3. Reasons, by class

The counts are hand tallies of the readers' reasons, checked against each slot's `plan.json` and texts.

### 3.1 What B1 actually gave the plan

| | A2 | A2′ | B1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| supporting people the plan saw | 29 | 29 | 21 (of 85 dealt; the pick chose "none" in F6_1, F6_3, F7_2) |
| … used at all (a job, the finale, or the question/answer) | 22 | 20 | 20 |
| … with a job of their own | 17 | 15 | 11 |
| … **in the plan's question or answer** | 9 | 7 | **15** |
| … power trades (toll-keeper, tax collector, bandit chief, moneylender, slaver, smuggler, landlord, reeve, magistrate, mercenary captain) | 7 | 7 | 12 (23 of 85 dealt) |
| keywords to the plan | 3 every time | 3 every time | 2 every time (the pick listed 4–6, never 1–3) |

- **The head count barely moved.** A2 used 22 supporting people across 24 sagas; B1 used 20. Dealing 3–4 and keeping one ends up with about as many people as A2's plan was already using.
- **What moved is which person, and where they sit.**
  - The pick chooses suspects: power trades are 12 of the 21 it kept, but only 23 of the 85 dealt. It kept no fisher out of 6 dealt.
  - The plan then puts the kept person into the question or the answer.

### 3.2 Keyword forced in: not removed

- **Count.** 6 of B1's 29 follow losses blame a keyword: F2_1 tavern (×2), F7_1 mule (×2), F8_1 eclipse/lighthouse (×2). That is 21%.
  - A2's losses to A0: 4 of 19 (21%).
  - A2′'s losses to A2: 4 of 26. Antler became a white-stag detour (F1_3); the stolen kite (F8_2). Both are slots where A2′'s pick differed from A2's.
- **Two keywords instead of three gave a misfit atom more of the story, not less.**
  - **F8_1, eclipse.** The pick ranked lighthouse and eclipse above mine and well. The plan's answer became a vault door "that opens only in the dark of an eclipse". The eclipse appears once in the texts, in the finale report: "She said the vault would open at the eclipse." Reader: "the lighthouse vault 'opening at the eclipse' never connect[s]."
  - **F7_1, mule.** The mule is both a job (steal the sailor's mule) and the answer ("a fine over a dead mule at market"). Reader: "brings in a 'fine over a dead mule' out of nowhere."
  - **F2_1, tavern.** The tavern became two places: the counting room under the Elmfen tavern, and a Rushmere tavern to hold. Reader: "I can't see why the tavern needs holding."
- **"Best first" does not protect the plan.** The pick puts the vivid atom first, not the one that fits best. Tavern was ranked first and still became a detour. Cutting to the top 2 cannot filter out a misfit the pick likes.
- **The build's watch item did not recur.** The horse race (F6_3) became the finale's event ("stop the merchant from fixing the horse race"), and B1 won all 4 reads there. A2′'s F6_3 also beat A2 in all 4 reads, so the earlier failure belonged to A2's draw, not to the keyword.
- **Root.** The forced keyword comes from the plan using every keyword it is given (plan.txt: "keywords: use each where it matters"). The number of keywords is not the cause.

### 3.3 Extra people: fewer errands, but a new class, N7, where the picked person takes over the question

- **Errands fell.** 11 supporting people had a job of their own (A2: 17). Only one slot reads as a side errand: F2_3's barn ("Y spends two jobs guarding a barn").
- **But the story now has two centres.** The kept person sits in the plan's question or answer in 15 of 21 sagas. The dealt opponent, the person the ending decides, is still there too.
  - **Villain swap (F8_2).** The question asks why the tax collector keeps raising the levy; the finale is against the merchant. Reader: "I'm sent after the tax collector, but the finale card suddenly names the merchant's 'hold on the levy'" (both orders).
  - **Ally turned culprit (F8_3, F1_1).**
    - The goatherd you win over is the one who sold the green oak: "the culprit is the goatherd who just joined my side, which feels arbitrary."
    - The fowler "sells them dear", and then the combs are "nearly worthless".
  - **A second layer of debt (F2_3, F5_1, F4_1).**
    - "I could not work out 'written over to the landlord as surety'."
    - "who owed Laudus what."
    - "Patty never gets her silver."
  - **The client's tie to the person is never given (F4_2, F7_1).**
    - The herbalist replaced A2's missing son: "we never learn why Flodoard cares about the herbalist."
    - The reeve is the prisoner, yet the saga "names the reeve only in the last line."
- **Count.** 12 of B1's 29 follow losses name the kept person, and 3 more describe their part without naming them. A2′'s 26 follow losses name a supporting person 4 times, and two of those are pronoun slips.
- **Why it happens.** The plan sees the kept person exactly as it sees any supporting person: the same fields and no label. The difference is how the person was chosen.
  - The pick chooses the person who fits one clear story, and that is usually a ready suspect.
  - The plan then builds its question on that suspect.
- **When it works, the person is the answer and the opponent is its face.** That happened in 4 of the 15:
  - F1_2: the toll-keeper hides coin in the antlers. "X's clue chain runs straight to the toll-keeper."
  - F4_3: the innkeeper is the missing person.
  - F5_2: the toll-keeper is the witness.
  - F7_3: the bandit chief's men burned the sailor's barges, so the dam is revenge.
- **A slot split is no evidence here.** B1 went 4–10 held on the 15 slots where the person took the question. But A2′, which has no kept person, went 5–9 on those same slots. Those are simply A2's strong slots (§1). The evidence for N7 is the readers' reasons, not that split.

## 4. Recommendation

1. **Keep `kit+pick` as the default.**
   - B1 shows no gain in any cut, and readers name a new failure that it brings (N7).
   - Leave B1 behind `SEED_ARM` as a lab arm. The shipped pick prompt renders byte-identical to before.
   - The pick word budget went from 80 to 85 only for B1's variant. Put it back to 80 if the B1 code is ever removed.
2. **Keep the content fixes.** These are: mercenary captain moved into COMPANY_TRADES, and distinct trades within one cast. They are content, not prompt changes.
   - They do change future kit deals compared with the recorded seed1 runs, so any re-run of A1–A4 must replay the old deals, as A2′ did.
3. **Principle learned: the plan builds on whatever the pipeline hands it as chosen.**
   - A picked keyword becomes a job or the answer. A picked person becomes the question.
   - Optional extras, such as A2's unpicked supporting people, stay at the edges or get dropped, and readers do not miss them (A2 dropped 7 of 29).
   - So the pipeline should hand the plan only what the story must be about: the situation, the client, the person the ending decides, and **one thing**.
4. **Next input-pipeline experiment (item 8): B2 "one object".**
   - **One change against A2.**
     - The pick lists keywords best first: B1's ranked pick without the people line.
     - The engine keeps only the top keyword.
     - The cast stays exactly as A2's: 0–2 supporting people, dealt, not picked. The plan prompt is unchanged.
   - **Why:**
     - "One thread, one object" is the readers' top follow credit in every arm.
     - In A1, a lone atom became the object of the story, and that worked (the pie, the brooch, the raven).
     - The misfit cases are the plan finding a use for every atom it gets. With one atom, that one use is the story itself.
     - B1 changed two things at once (people and keyword count), so it cannot say which one mattered. B2 tests the count alone.
   - **Risks (watched):**
     - Fewer atoms means fewer odd hooks, so keep may drop.
     - The top atom can still be a vivid misfit: F8_1 ranked lighthouse first.
   - **Method (from §1):**
     - Replay A2's deals, as A2′ did, so the deal is not a confound.
     - Judge B2 against **both A2 and A2′** (96 reads), so the incumbent's draw cancels out.
     - Count it as a gain only if it wins follow against both, or if the pooled held margin clears about ±12 of the 48 slot-pairs.
   - **If B2 lands inside the floor too,** the next step is more slots (a seed2 of 24 new slots), not another arm on seed1. A real effect the size of A2's wins (about 12–7 held) needs around 100 slots before a single run shows it beyond the floor more often than not.
5. **Alternative, needs the designer: B3 "lean cast" (no supporting people at all).**
   - For: A2's best saga (F4_2) had none, and A2 left 7 of its 29 dealt people unused with no cost readers could see.
   - Against: it departs from item 7's seed design, which includes 1–3 potential supporting people. So it waits for a ruling.
