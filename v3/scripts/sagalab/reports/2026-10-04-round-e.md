**What the two failed rounds show.**
- **Five one-change arms after C2 (D1, D2, E1, E2, E3), none ahead.** Each fixed or moved its own slice: D1's doer, D2's shortfalls, E3's shortfalls, E2's state contradictions; E1 made its slice worse. No slice was big enough to show at 72 pairs.

  - reveal or motive does not add up: 42–46% of G0's losses;
  - set up nowhere: 25–27%;
  - side swaps: 17–25%.
- **Two laws sharpen this round:**
  - **One author per fact.** A step that rewrites a fact other parts were built on breaks them. E1 rewrote the finale under the plan's title, edges and buttons.
  - **Dependency order.** A fact must be written after everything it depends on. E2 wrote each clue before the job that turns it up, and the jobs could not deliver it. This is the fourth time the law about steps that fix story facts has been measured, now within a single call.

**The bigger change (item 8: shape the input and the steps, not the wording): F1, "the page first".**
- **What changes.** The plan writes its facts in dependency order, still in one call and by one author:
  1. the question;
  2. the jobs as the player will see them. Each names **whom it meets face to face** and **what it turns up**.
  3. the answer, built **only from the people met and the things turned up** in those jobs, plus the asker;
  4. each job's clue, made from what that job turned up and pointing at the answer;
  5. the hopes (whys);
  6. the finale.
- **What stays.** The answer is still written before the clues (law 6d holds). The road ahead, the engine-written buttons, card 1 as its own call and the quest log are unchanged.
- **Why this is bigger.** It changes *what the answer is allowed to be made of* and the order the saga is authored in. It is not another wording pass. It goes straight at the classes that every recent arm and the default share:
  - set up nowhere: a culprit the player never met cannot be in the answer if the answer may only use people the jobs meet;
  - reveal not adding up: each clue is made from a thing its own job turned up.
- **Engine checks, log-only (single-shot ruling):**
  - every cast person the answer names is met in a job before the finale;
  - each learn names something its own job turns up.
  - Compare both rates against G0's plans, which are already on disk.
- **Prompt budget.** The plan prompt's worst variant is at 587 of 620 words. Adding `meets` / `turns up` means merging or cutting lines first (CLAUDE.md, PROMPT_RULES §0).
- **Risk.**
  - Answers built from what is on the page may get plainer, so watch keep. Readability ranks first, but a keep loss beyond the floor blocks the change.
  - If Sonnet ignores the order inside one call, the fallback is the same split as two calls: jobs first, then meaning, with the second call seeing the first call's whole output. Both calls stay single-shot.
- **Power.** Three generations against G0's three draws (on disk).
  - It counts as a gain only if:
    - the pooled follow held margin is beyond +15, **or** it is ahead in all three generations;
    - set-up-nowhere blame falls by more than 15 points, and its mechanical check falls;
    - keep does not fall beyond the floor.
  - Before judging, run the zero-context verifier on the full rendered plan prompt.
- **The designer's queued directions (item 0)** are a different kind of bigger change. They are personal-saga arms (state the past plainly, change the soldier, write it back into the dossier) and texture and pacing arms (room, dialogue, lore, against Sultan's pacing). Both target story quality and can run on G0 independently of F1. Do not combine either with F1 in one arm.
