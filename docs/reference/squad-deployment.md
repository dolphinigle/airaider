# Squad deployment: what makes sending the squad out fun

Synthesis of three research sweeps (E-0002, 2026-10-20) across about 40 games: dispatch and auto-resolve systems, tactical squad games, and dice, gambling and auto-battler resolution. Full reports with sources are in [E-0002/samples](../../experiments/E-0002-what-makes-squad-deployment-fun/samples/).

## The answer in one line

Gambling-style resolution can carry the fun, but only when **every roll is the payoff of a player decision**. Tactical combat buys two things that dice can't: puzzle mastery, and losses that feel like your own fault. Players praise everything else in these games, and all of it already works as plain rolls (Darkest Dungeon, Mordheim's injury roll, Kenshi, RimWorld, Wildermyth, Oregon Trail, Balatro, Can't Stop).

## What tactics give, and the dice substitute

| Fun source | Needs tactics? | Dice substitute |
|---|---|---|
| Puzzle mastery, moment-to-moment improvisation | yes | none; rarely what players retell |
| Owning the risk ("every death your own fault") | partly | choices around each roll: who, approach, push, retreat |
| Tension on a named person | no | visible odds over a merc you know |
| Surprise | no | a varied event pool where who you sent changes the event |
| Consequence and attachment | no | wounds, scars, traits, bonds, death that stick to the person |

## Principles (each found independently in two or three of the sweeps)

1. **A decision before every roll, or cut the roll.** Who goes, which approach, which trait to lean on, how much to spend. A roll nobody influenced is narration with a number on it.
2. **Stop for decisions mid-run.** Oregon Trail, Banner Saga, Dispatch, Darkest Dungeon: resolution runs on its own until something happens, then asks. Zero input during the run is the known failure.
3. **Push your luck.** After each encounter: bank the loot and come home, or go deeper for more. Darkest Dungeon removed a risk-free option because "all the tension was lost."
4. **A push lever on the roll (Blades in the Dark).** Buy better odds with something that matters later: a wound, fatigue, gold, or a complication.
5. **Show odds and stakes before the roll.** "80%; on a fail Atlas is wounded and loses the pack." If failure costs nothing nameable, don't roll. Hidden math reads as cheating.
6. **Three result bands; failure is content.** Success / success at a cost / failure. Failure changes the squad or opens a new situation (an ambush becomes a rescue). "Quest failed" with nothing changed is dead air.
7. **Rolls feed each other.** A wound from roll 1 lowers roll 2 and sharpens the next push-or-retreat call. Independent flips in a row are one flip repeated.
8. **Tune odds to feeling.** Players read 80–85% as certain. Keep rolls that carry stakes around 40–75%, and save long shots for gambles the player chose. Smooth bad streaks quietly by lowering variance; never fake the shown number.
9. **Who you sent changes the event.** FTL's blue options, Dispatch's hero-specific choices: a trait unlocks an option, not just a bonus. Events that ignore who went are used up in a few runs.
10. **Sending costs something.** The merc is gone for days, can't take the other quest, comes back hurt. Free sends make the choice meaningless.
11. **Named people, lasting marks.** The risk falls on someone the player knows, and every quest leaves a mark on them (XP, scar, quirk, bond, death). Faceless troops become fodder.
12. **Show something while it resolves.** Oregon Trail's designer found the numbers "rather boring" and the moving wagon "a huge winner"; Dispatch's radio banter is its most-praised part. A timer followed by a report reads as padding.
13. **Spend time in proportion to stakes.** Players auto-resolve obvious fights and lean in when someone they value is at risk.

## Failure modes

- **Spectator mode:** watching rolls you had no hand in.
- **Foregone outcomes:** high odds and a high win rate (E-0001 run 1: 3/3 at 65–80%).
- **Blank failure:** the quest fails and nothing changes.
- **Samey events:** a thin pool, or events that ignore who went.
- **Slot-machine drift:** many fast rolls with no decisions, a compulsive "again."
- **Narration carrying the fun:** against D-0004. Test: is it still interesting with the text removed?
