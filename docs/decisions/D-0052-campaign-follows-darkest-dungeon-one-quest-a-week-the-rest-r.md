## Context

Combat already follows Darkest Dungeon (D-0041) and includes stress (D-0042). The designer wants the campaign to follow it too.

## Decision

The designer's words: "i think we want to model this game more to Darkest Dungeon model. So instead of flurry of quests, you usually only take one quest per week. The rest of the team rests, gets stress relief, etc. Gold sinks are those: stress relief, injury healing (replace wounded), and base buildings. Some ideas from v3 will work here, e.g., the merc level cap is capped by their room prestige and stuff which will be gold sinks. The quests initially will only bring like 1-2 mercs, and lasts for 1-2 battles. later on with 4 merc, it can go like 4 battles and 1 boss battle or something."

- **A week is the turn.** The player usually takes **one quest per week**.
- **The rest of the roster rests:** stress relief, healing and the like.
- **Gold sinks:**
  - stress relief;
  - **injury healing** (this replaces the "wounded" state);
  - base buildings.
- **A merc's level cap comes from their room** (prestige), as in v3, where a bedroom's comfort capped its owner's level. Raising it costs gold.
- **Quests grow with the campaign:**
  - early, 1–2 mercs and 1–2 battles;
  - later, 4 mercs and about 4 battles plus a boss.

## Open (Claude's review, 2026-10-22)

- **Pushing on or retreating between battles.** This is Darkest Dungeon's core tension, the player's one decision inside a quest. It is priced and always open (findings: retreat always open, always priced, tiered).
- **Camping** in long quests (E-0005 round 3).
- **The campaign clock:** what makes a week matter (quests that expire, threats that grow, a season or end date).
- **Keeping coin flips frequent** (vision): a quest packs its battles with skill checks between them, so the fewer quests still carry many flips.
- **Who goes:** each quest states how many mercs it takes, from 1 to 4.
- **Recovery time:** injured mercs sit out a number of weeks unless gold speeds it up.
