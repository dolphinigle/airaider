# Unified vs. parallel engines for non-combat challenges: research report

## Short answer

The games that handle this well mostly run one resolution core and change the structure around it according to the stakes. They do not build separate engines per domain. Fate, Burning Wheel, Mouse Guard/Torchbearer, Blades and Disco Elysium all use the same dice for fights, talk and knowledge. What changes is the shape of the encounter: how many exchanges, what can be lost, and who acts. Griftlands is the main parallel-engine case. Its negotiation was praised as a game but criticized as a reskin of combat that sits apart from the conversation it is supposed to represent. On the specific worry about an axe-user failing a "Light Weapons" test, the strongest pattern in these sources is that a test names a goal or approach. The character then brings the skill that fits, and how well it fits changes the odds or the effect.

## 1. Case studies

### Blades in the Dark: one roll for every action, combat included
- **Mechanic.** The player picks the action rating that matches what the character is doing in the fiction ("You can't roll a given action rating unless your character is presently performing that action in the fiction"). The GM sets **position** (controlled / risky / desperate) and **effect** (limited / standard / great). A 6 is a clean success, 4/5 is success with a cost, and 1–3 is failure. There is no separate combat system. In a fight, one roll covers both sides: on a 4/5 both sides get their effect ([action roll SRD](https://bladesinthedark.com/action-roll)).
- **How fit is handled.** The same Skirmish roll changes with the setup. A wild charge into a gang worsens position and reduces effect. Fighting from a choke point keeps the risk down but effect stays reduced ([Roll20 compendium](https://roll20.net/compendium/BITD/Setting%20Position%20and%20Effect)). Fit changes the stakes; it does not decide whether you may roll at all.
- **Failure leaves a mark.** There are five consequence types: reduced effect, complication (ticks on a clock), lost opportunity, worse position, and harm in four levels ([consequences](https://bladesinthedark.com/consequences-harm)). The player can push for an extra die at a cost of 2 stress or take a Devil's Bargain ([action roll](https://bladesinthedark.com/action-roll)).
- **The squad-level roll.** The engagement roll is a fortune roll that sets the starting position of the whole operation. It starts at 1d, +1d for a bold plan, +1d if the chosen "detail" (point of attack, method of deception, route) hits the target's weakness, and −1d if the target is strongest against that approach. The result is controlled, risky or desperate. The rules say that "a desperate position is the worst thing that can result" from planning ([planning & engagement](https://bladesinthedark.com/planning-engagement)).
- **Knowledge.** Common knowledge is handed over with no roll. If something stands in the way, it is an action roll; otherwise a fortune roll sets *quality*. Great effect gives "exceptional details", limited gives "incomplete or partial information" ([gathering information](https://bladesinthedark.com/gathering-information)). The roll decides how much you learn, not whether you learn anything.
- **Reception.** Citizen Sleeper's designer points to Blades because it "doesn't separate combat out from everything else" ([Rascal](https://www.rascal.news/tracing-citizen-sleepers-circuitous-vector-from-tabletop-to-hit-video-game/)). The usual complaint is thin tactics: some groups house-rule the action and resistance rules, and combat-built characters can roll big pools ([G+ archive](https://bitd.gplusarchive.online/?p=144165)).

### Fate Core: same dice, four structures
- **Four actions:** overcome, create an advantage, attack, defend ([SRD](https://fate-srd.com/fate-core/four-actions)).
- **The structure ladder.**
  - **Single overcome roll.** On a tie you succeed at a minor cost; on a failure you either fail or "succeed at a serious cost."
  - **Challenge.** Several overcome rolls for a complicated situation, where "each overcome action uses a different skill to deal with one task." The GM decides nothing about the outcome "until after you've collected all the results." There are no attacks in a challenge ([challenges](https://fate-srd.com/fate-core/challenges)).
  - **Contest.** Mutually exclusive goals with no harm intended. Each exchange's winner scores a victory and first to three wins. A tie produces a twist ([contests](https://fate-srd.com/fate-core/contests)).
  - **Conflict.** Characters have "the intent and the ability to harm one another." Harm can be physical or mental ([conflicts](https://fate-srd.com/fate-core/conflicts)).
- **How knowledge works.** Lore can overcome obstacles and can create advantages "provided you can research the subject in question," but "Lore isn't used in conflicts" ([Lore](https://fate-srd.com/fate-core/lore)). Create-an-advantage covers "learning a monster's weakness through research" and grants free invocations, which are bonuses for later rolls ([four actions](https://fate-srd.com/fate-core/four-actions)). Knowledge pays off as fuel for a later test, not as a pass/fail gate.
- **Philosophy.** The Bronze Rule says anything can be treated like a character, which saves writing new subsystems ([Toolkit](https://fate-srd.com/fate-system-toolkit/bronze-rule)).

### Mouse Guard / Torchbearer: one conflict engine, different skills per action
- **Mechanic.** Each side secretly scripts three actions from Attack, Defend, Feint and Maneuver, revealed one at a time. A matrix decides whether each pairing is an independent or versus test. Maneuver results scale with successes: −1D to the opponent, then +2D for you, then disarm ([crib sheet](https://mk-rpg.org.uk/Mouse_Guard_crib_sheet)). The action cards cover "any type of conflict" ([Roll20 forum](https://app.roll20.net/forum/permalink/3779/)).
- **Different skills per conflict type.** Mouse Guard's table pairs Argument with Persuader, Speech with Orator, Negotiation with Haggler, and Journey with Pathfinder (an unofficial transcription: [scribd](https://www.scribd.com/document/57405482/Thom)). Torchbearer's types include Kill, Capture, Drive Off, Convince, Convince Crowd, Trick/Riddle, Pursue/Flee and Banish ([forum summary](https://www.unseenservant.us/forum/viewtopic.php?p=280155)).
- **Different skills per action.** Usually Attack and Feint share one skill and Defend and Maneuver share another. Capture and Convince break that pattern: defending in a Capture uses *Hunter*, because "a Hunter understands the ways of prey" ([BW forum errata thread](https://forums.burningwheel.com/t/errata-conflict-ability-and-skills-used-by-action/13714)). In Convince, "weapons" are things like intimidation, evidence or promises ([BW forum](https://forums.burningwheel.com/t/ogre/13043)). This is the most direct answer to the axe-user problem: inside one conflict, several skills each get a role.
- **Outcome.** Winners usually owe a compromise scaled to how hurt they were, and the compromise is negotiated, "not GM Fiat" ([BW forum](https://forums.burningwheel.com/t/a-few-gm-questions/10242)).
- **Reception.** Reviewers split. One calls it "elegant and minimalist"; another calls it "a byzantine combination of … dice-rolling … and Rock-Paper-Scissors" that is "inescapable" because every complicated encounter uses it ([Shut Up & Sit Down](https://www.shutupandsitdown.com/review-mouse-guard), [RPGnet](https://dev.rpg.net/reviews/archive/14/14195.phtml)).

### Burning Wheel: Duel of Wits and Fight!, used at different zoom levels
- **Duel of Wits.** Both sides state stakes before the duel. The result "is binding, but … according to the context established before" it started ([BW forum](https://forums.burningwheel.com/t/thoughts-on-duel-of-wits-social-skills-and-player-control/23701)). Compromise depends on how much of the winner's Body of Argument is left. Players "get into Duel of Wits that they know they will lose just to get a compromise" ([BW forum](https://forums.burningwheel.com/t/duel-of-wits-itll-happen-but-how-much-will-it-cost/12474)).
- **Known flaws.** Point/Dismiss dominate unless the stakes make even a compromise ugly ([same thread](https://forums.burningwheel.com/t/duel-of-wits-itll-happen-but-how-much-will-it-cost/12474)). Players find feints hard to roleplay ([forum](https://forums.burningwheel.com/t/some-questions-about-duel-of-wits/13286)), and some find the scripting "weirdly artificial" ([ENWorld](https://www.enworld.org/threads/grading-the-burning-wheel-system.702810/)).
- **Zoom levels.** A simple versus test, then Bloody Versus, then Fight!. Fight! is used "when everyone is involved and when it's important to the players in terms of their beliefs." Bloody Versus fits when the intent is only "to get past them/drive them away" ([BW forum](https://forums.burningwheel.com/t/bloody-versus-for-blood/23929/5)). Players call Duel of Wits "a zoomed-in version of simple versus tests" ([forum](https://forums.burningwheel.com/t/thoughts-on-duel-of-wits-social-skills-and-player-control/23701)).
- **Design intent.** Luke Crane wanted conflict systems "ripe with uncertainty and adversity" with "many meaningful choices" ([interview](https://iimu.kapsi.fi/limunropellukset/archives/2726)).

### D&D 4e skill challenges: what went wrong
- **Math.** Reaching X successes before 3 failures magnifies the per-roll odds. Justin Alexander puts the break point at about **65% per check**: below it, more complex challenges become nearly unwinnable, and PCs had "about a 10–20% chance of succeeding on a skill challenge designed for their level." Errata fixed only the worst cases ([The Alexandrian](https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/1971/roleplaying-games/playtesting-4th-edition-part-6-skill-challenges)). A ±1 DC change could move success from 50% to 10% or 90% ([ENWorld](https://www.enworld.org/goto/post?id=4382792)).
- **Best skill spam.** The system "rewards the group for figuring out whoever has the highest applicable skill modifier" and having that character roll everything ([Alexandrian](https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/1971/roleplaying-games/playtesting-4th-edition-part-6-skill-challenges)). One proposed fix limits which skills apply in each "arena" ([ENWorld](https://www.enworld.org/threads/rethinking-skill-challenges.278377/)).
- **Disconnected from the world.** The challenge tracks *how much* the party did, not *what* it did. The common verdict is "a string of dice rolls" ([ENWorld](https://www.enworld.org/threads/4e-skill-challenges-an-exercise-in-dice-rolling.246093/)).
- **What Alexander keeps.** Letting different skills feed one complex check encourages cooperation better than Aid Another does.

### Savage Worlds: light parallel structures plus cross-domain Tests
- **Dramatic Task.** Each participant is dealt an action card and the party needs a set number of successes before time runs out. A Club card gives −2, and a failed roll on a Club fails the whole task. Combat and task cards stay separate.
- **Social Conflict.** Three rounds, one point argued per round, Persuasion against Spirit. Each success or raise earns an Influence token, and the total token count is read off a result table.
- **Quick Encounter.** Each player picks one skill, and the outcome is partial and narrated. All three are summarized in [Saving Throw Show](https://savingthrowshow.com/?p=8066).
- **Tests.** Any skill, such as Taunt, Intimidation or Athletics, can be rolled in combat against the linked attribute to make a foe Distracted or Vulnerable, and Shaken on a raise. Pinnacle reworked Tests because they had been "fun but not as mechanically useful as we'd like" ([Pinnacle](https://peginc.com/?p=144137)). This is a clean way to bring non-combat skills into fights.

### Griftlands: negotiation as a parallel card battle
- **Mechanic.** A separate negotiation deck. Resolve plays the role of HP and carries over between negotiations. Composure plays the role of block. You attack the opponent's core argument and side arguments ([GameSkinny](https://www.gameskinny.com/sqa55/griftlands-guide-what-are-negotiations-and-how-do-they-work)).
- **Praise.** Game Informer called negotiation "excellent in flavor and execution" ([GI](https://gameinformer.com/review/griftlands/griftlands-review-great-friends-in-low-places)). The interplay worked: "Reducing a character's morale via negotiation, then going in for the kill." Killing has reputation costs, while a persuaded enemy "may become a nuisance down the road" ([GameCritics](https://gamecritics.com/mike-suskie/griftlands-review/)).
- **Critique: a reskin.** "Little mechanical difference between the physical battles and the verbal ones … It's only the names of the cards that are different." Also: "the cards you play don't actually represent a cohesive argument," and the duel "is conducted entirely separately from the dialogue system" ([bit-tech](https://bit-tech.net/reviews/gaming/pc/griftlands-review/1/)). The Xbox Hub: "Composure … is effectively just defence," and arguments are "just characters with their own lifepoints" ([Xbox Hub](https://www.thexboxhub.com/griftlands-review/)).
- **Critique: binary and disconnected.** PC Gamer says the game "never quite bridges the space between the actual dialogue and the card game," which reduces interactions to success or failure. It points to *Signs of the Sojourner*, which "much more cleverly uses its mechanics to shape and fuel character interactions" ([PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/griftlands-review)). Signs' conversations are shape matching, with hand and deck limits forcing you to sacrifice cards or conversations ([NWR](https://www.nintendoworldreport.com/review/56569/signs-of-the-sojourner-switch-review)).
- **Incentive imbalance.** Talk was safer and paid better, so "there's little incentive to do more battle than negotiations," and negotiation "itself is a form of combat, so it could feel tiring" ([Klei forums](https://kleiforums.com/forums/topic/109066-the-game-is-too-biased-towards-negotiations/)).

### Disco Elysium: all skills, no combat system
- **Checks.** 2d6 + skill + modifiers against a difficulty. White checks can be retried after you raise the skill; red checks are one-shot ([gfinity](https://gfinity.net/disco-elysium/can-you-fail/)). Earlier choices add modifiers to later checks ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/632470/discussions/0/3205995441640481281)). Passive checks put lore into dialogue unprompted. Their opacity is criticized: "there is never an indication" of what a low-Intellect build missed ([Backloggd](https://backloggd.com/u/heychrisfox/)).
- **Combat as checks.** The devblog describes "whirls", pseudo-turns where time freezes so you can consult your skills. Attack (Hand/Eye) and dodge (Reaction Speed) are red checks where "both the negative and positive outcomes are played out." Passives change the active check: Visual Calculus spots an angle, Half Light urges you to run. "Researching an enemy's equipment … can give a large bonus." It is "a one-size-fits-all solution for action scenes," and "there is no disconnection between story and combat" ([devblog](https://discoelysium.com/devblog/2018/05/03/combat-in-disco-elysium)).
- **On failure,** Kurvitz says: "You never prevent them from accessing a part of the game. A mistake should … make you feel embarrassed, afraid, regretful" ([search summary of Canard PC interview via GameBanshee](https://gamebanshee.com/3wkrx)).

### King of Dragon Pass / Six Ages: choosing who goes
- **Mechanic.** Choices succeed or fail on "hidden tests of skills" ([IF50](https://if50.substack.com/p/1999-king-of-dragon-pass)). Outcomes depend on "the skills of your leaders, your relationships with other clans, and simple chance" ([FAQ](https://a-sharp.com/kodp/ifaq.html)). The developer's tip on bandits attacking a caravan: "can your trader fight?" Small-unit combat opens "like rock/scissors/paper that affects the odds" ([tips](https://a-sharp.com/kodp/tips.html)). Here a combat skill shows up in a trade event.
- **Advisors.** In Six Ages, advisors exist mainly "to provide information": they predict consequences and recommend options. Personality hints at execution ("a kindly character may be a poor choice for intimidation"), and advice the game cannot back up "undermines the entire idea of useful advice" ([Six Ages blog](https://blog.sixages.com/?p=714)). The fan wiki says most challenges use the highest relevant skill on the circle ([wiki](https://sixages.fandom.com/wiki/Clan_Circle)).
- **Consequences** reach years ahead: Dunham wanted decisions to "matter ten years later" ([IF50](https://if50.substack.com/p/1999-king-of-dragon-pass)).

### Sultan's Game: card slots and a dice pool
- **Mechanic.** Required (red) slots must be filled before a rite starts; optional slots add help, often Intel cards. The pool is the sum of the listed attributes, each die is a binary success, and difficulty sets how many faces succeed. Opposed checks subtract the opponent's attributes from your pool. Gold dice are auto-successes; rerolls redo the whole pool ([wiki](https://sultansgame.wiki.gg/wiki/Ritual)). Combat-like and social rites use the same check with different attributes. One player's notes list "Physique/Survival check – 10 success" ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/3117820/discussions/0/604153852924567547)).
- **Critique.** It is often unclear how a card in a given slot will affect the result ([GameGeeker](https://gamegeeker.com/games/sultan-s-game-3117820?market=US)), and the dice UI is hard to follow ([Boss Rush](https://bossrush.net/2025/04/game-review-sultans-game/)).

### Darkest Dungeon curios: the item is the skill
- **Mechanic.** Each curio has a by-hand outcome table. The Sarcophagus, for example: 60% gold or heirlooms, 20% a negative quirk, 20% nothing. Using the right provision (torch, key, holy water, shovel…) changes the table and often guarantees the result. Quirks like Curious or Kleptomaniac *force* interactions. The Antiquarian class gets extra loot from curios ([wiki](https://darkestdungeon.wiki.gg/wiki/Curio)). The knowledge check is the player's own memory of which item goes where, paid for in supplies, and who you brought changes the outcome.

### Citizen Sleeper: dice allocation
- **Mechanic.** Dice are rolled at the start of each cycle, and the number of dice drops as your condition worsens ([GIGAZINE](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20240309-citizen-sleeper)). The decision is which die goes where; the risk is shown before you commit. A 6 always succeeds; 3–4 give 25% positive / 50% neutral / 25% negative; in the sequel, off-skill crew lose die value ([TBL](https://turnbasedlovers.com/review/citizen-sleeper-2-starward-vector-review), [Sixth Axis](https://thesixthaxis.com/2025/02/17/citizen-sleeper-2-starward-vector-review)). Hacking wants *low* dice, so bad rolls still have uses ([Escapist](https://www.escapistmagazine.com/citizen-sleeper-makes-simple-dice-rolls-endlessly-gripping/)). The Intuition skill doesn't raise odds; it shows them ([GIGAZINE](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20240309-citizen-sleeper)). Clocks create time pressure.

### Side note: Loop Hero
Combat accepts no input. Reviewers call the player a "combat voyeur" whose agency sits in loadout and world-building ([RPGamer](https://rpgamer.com/2021/03/loop-hero-deep-look/)). The main decision is the retreat: keep 100% of resources if you retreat at camp, 60% mid-loop, 30% on death ([Slant](https://www.slantmagazine.com/games/loop-hero-review/)).

## 2. Cross-cutting answers

**When is one unified engine better than two?**
- **When fit and effort should feel the same everywhere.** Blades, Fate, Torchbearer and Disco Elysium put fights and talk on equal footing, so a talker or a scholar is as much a protagonist as a fighter. Disco Elysium's devs claim "no disconnection between story and combat."
- **When stakes vary, scale the structure, not the engine.** Fate's overcome, challenge, contest and conflict all use the same dice. So do Burning Wheel's simple, Bloody Versus and Fight!. Use the heavy structure only when "it's important to the players."
- **Two engines pay off only if their verbs truly differ and they feed each other.** Griftlands' cross-feed (soften with talk, then kill) was praised. Its identical verbs were the main critique.

**When does a talk-battle feel forced?**
- It feels forced when talk is combat with renamed numbers: HP called resolve, block called composure. It also feels forced when the mini-game runs beside the dialogue instead of being the dialogue, when it collapses to win/lose, and when it pays better than fighting so the other mode withers.
- It works when both sides set stakes beforehand and the loser gets a compromise (Duel of Wits, Mouse Guard), and when the verbs represent the act itself (Signs of the Sojourner's shape matching).

**How do you make a lore/knowledge check interesting?**
1. **Never gate progress on it.** GUMSHOE hands over core clues with no roll, because missed rolls make play "grind to a halt" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gumshoe_System)). Kurvitz: never lock players out.
2. **The roll sets quality, not access.** Blades: great, standard or limited detail.
3. **Knowledge as fuel.** Fate's create-an-advantage gives free invokes; Disco Elysium's research gives "a large bonus" in a fight; Blades' plan detail that hits a weakness gives +1d.
4. **A wrong answer costs something.** Fate's failed advantage gives the opponent a free invoke. Darkest Dungeon's by-hand gamble can give a negative quirk.
5. **Advice as the knowledge payoff.** Six Ages' advisors predict consequences; knowledge shows what each option risks.

**How do combat skills show up outside fights, and the reverse?**
- **Combat skills in non-combat events:** KoDP's "can your trader fight?"; Disco Elysium's Physique skills in dialogue options; Torchbearer's Hunter for defending in a Capture.
- **Non-combat skills in fights:** Savage Worlds Tests (Taunt to make a foe Distracted); Disco Elysium's Visual Calculus and Rhetoric inside whirls; Fate's Provoke for mental attacks.
- **Mechanisms that avoid the "wrong weapon" fail:** Blades lets the player choose the action and judges fit through position and effect. Torchbearer assigns a different skill to each action. Six Ages uses the best skill present. Citizen Sleeper's off-skill penalty lowers die value instead of forbidding the action.

## 3. Implications for airaider

- **Keep one coin engine for everything.** Every success story in this research uses one resolution core with structures scaled to stakes. Griftlands is the main parallel engine, and its "reskin" and "disconnected" critiques are the risk an autobattler-for-fights would run.
- **Tests should name a goal and an approach, not one core skill.** "Break the gate guard (Fighting)" lets the merc bring their best Fighting core. Fit can add or remove coins, like Blades' effect or Citizen Sleeper's off-skill −die. The axe-user brings Heavy Weapons and loses nothing.
- **For fights, consider a Torchbearer-style conflict before an autobattler.** Two or three exchanges on the same coins, each exchange drawing a different skill (strike: weapon core; hold: Defense or Athletics; trick: Roguery or Warfare). Between exchanges, give the player a decision such as push, swap who's in front, or retreat. If you do build an autobattler, follow Loop Hero: put decisions at the seams (before the fight and at retreat points with tiered loss like 100/60/30) and show the odds.
- **Fold non-combat skills into fights.** A Savage Worlds-style Test: Intimidation, Performance or Beasts used mid-fight to remove enemy coins. That makes "who you sent" change the fight.
- **Lore and Knowing cores create advantage.** A success adds coins to a later test, reveals a weakness, or turns a hidden failure mode into a shown one. Do not gate progress on them; lore success decides how much you learn (Blades' quality tiers).
- **Watch out for success thresholds.** The 4e math applies directly to "quest needs N successes." Chained tests amplify per-test odds. Alexander's ~65% pivot means 40–60% tests in a long chain collapse overall success. Simulate the quest-level odds, not the per-test odds, and show the quest-level odds.
- **Stop one merc from taking every test.** Use Fate's rule (each roll in a challenge uses a different skill) or a 4e-style arena limit, so the best merc can't take every test. Squad breadth then matters.
- **Every failure is one of Blades' five consequence types:** reduced effect, complication tick, lost opportunity, worse position, or harm. That gives failure a mark without dead ends.
- **Talk conflicts end in a compromise.** Use Duel of Wits / Mouse Guard: stakes stated first, the loser still gets something scaled by how close it was. Don't give talk its own HP bar.
- **Show odds before commitment.** Citizen Sleeper's previewed outcome spread and Six Ages' advisors are both examples. Mercs could comment on odds in their own voice (cheap flavor text, real information), but the advice must match the actual mechanics.
- **Balance incentives across approaches.** Griftlands' talk paid better and cost less, so fighting withered. Each approach needs a distinct cost profile: fights risk wounds, talk risks favors or reputation, stealth risks time or discovery.
- **Let gear and items act as knowledge.** Darkest Dungeon's "right provision fixes the table": carried supplies or a merc's background (Antiquarian-like) can change a test's outcome table, which is visible, readable and squad-dependent.
